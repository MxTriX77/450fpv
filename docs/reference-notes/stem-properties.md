### 2.2 The critical speed as a law (derived)

The published numbers fit one picture: a blade cuts a free stem only if the stem's inertia can push back with the cutting force before the stem moves away. A first-principles estimate (derived here; not from the sources):

- **The stem as an infinite Euler–Bernoulli beam struck at a point.** Its drive-point mobility is v/F = (1 − i)/(4 μ c_B), with μ the mass per length and c_B = (EI/μ)^¼ ω^½ the bending wave speed (the standard result for an infinite beam). So the force a stem can oppose to a blade moving at v is |F| ≈ 2√2 (EI μ³)^¼ ω^½ v.
- **The cut takes t = δ/v,** δ ≈ d the stem's depth across the blade's path, so ω ≈ π v/δ.
- **The edge needs F_c ≈ e_s·A/δ** on average over the cut (e_s the specific cutting energy, A the section area).
- **Critical speed:** v_c = [F_c √δ / (2√(2π) (EI μ³)^¼)]^(2/3).
- **For a solid section** this scales as v_c ∝ e_s^(2/3) E^(−1/6) ρ^(−1/2) d^(−2/3): thicker, denser stems are cut at lower speed, the edge's cutting force counts most (power 2/3), stiffness least (power 1/6).
- **Valid while** the bending wavelength over the cut time, λ = 2π (EI/(μ ω²))^¼, is shorter than the stem's free length above the cut and its distance to a support. Within about λ of a support the stem is held and cut more slowly.

Checks against the published values:
- **Grass culms:** 15–23 m/s sits on Tuck et al.'s 20–27 m/s best case [11] and under their 35 m/s for a stem 10 mm from support.
- **Support distance:** λ/2 of a grass culm is 7–16 mm, the same order as [11]'s "10 mm or more from the ledger" threshold.
- **Thick stalks:** a 20 mm solid stalk gives 4.8 m/s, the order of the sorghum stalk's 2.9–3.5 m/s optimum and 5–7 m/s full efficiency [9], and of Feller's 1.5–9.8 m/s [9].
- **Unsupported crop cutting:** [12]'s 60–80 m/s and [14]'s 61–86 m/s line speeds lie above the sharp-edge figures by 3–4×. The difference is the edge, next.

**The edge** (derived and assumed). F_c rises steeply with the edge's bluntness, and v_c with F_c^(2/3).
- A sharp mower blade cuts grass from about 25 m/s ([13], [11]). A round nylon line of radius 0.44 mm needs 86 m/s, of 0.83 mm 71 m/s [14]. The line also bends and carries its own momentum, so this is a bound, not an edge law.
- **The prop's leading edge is round:** its radius r = 1.1019 (t/c)² c (the NACA four-digit nose radius, [15]) from `drone.json`'s own blade table gives 0.65 mm at 0.40 R (c 23.3, t 3.70 mm), **0.30 mm at 0.74 R** (c 14.65, t 2.01 mm) and 0.13 mm at the tip (derived). That is the thinnest trimmer line's radius, not a knife's.
- So the prop's edge factor k_edge = v_c(prop)/v_c(sharp) is **2–4 (assumed)**, between the sharp blade and the line: green culms and straw are cut only above about **35–90 m/s**, which puts the line between "cut" and "pushed aside" inside the outer half of the disc (86–116 m/s past 0.74 R). Weed stalks (sharp 6–13 m/s, so 12–50 m/s with the edge) are cut wherever the blade reaches them.
- **Lean and rake raise it:** a stem leaning 65° or more, or a rake over 60°, needs over 80 m/s even with a mower's blade [11]. A stem bent over by the downwash (section 2.4) meets the blade leaning, so it escapes more often.

### 2.3 Impact against quasi-static cutting energy

- The quasi-static e_s of straw (18.8–25.7 kJ/m², audit §5.1) is the edge's own work.
- Impact tests add the stem's deflection and the severed part's kinetic energy. [9] shows energy falling to a minimum near 3 m/s, then rising as the severed parts are flung. [10] measured 83–121 kJ/m² at 8–11 m/s on a woody grass, against the 4.6 J quasi-static restated in [10] (its section not given, so no ratio is derived).
- **Derived recommendation:** take the edge's work as e_s·A (quasi-static) and model the fling (m_f·v², audit §5.2) and the stem's bending separately, as 2.2's design already does. A separate impact multiplier would count the fling twice.

## 5. Still assumed

- **The original impact-cutting papers were not read.** O'Dogherty & Gale 1986 (J. Agric. Eng. Res. 35: 115–129), McRandal & McNulty 1978 (J. Agric. Eng. Res. 23: 313–328) and 1980 (Trans. ASAE 23: 816–821) and Persson's book: the publishers refused automated reads, and the catalogue records (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, ASABE) carry no abstract or no numbers. Search-engine summaries attribute 15–30 m/s and 20 m/s critical speeds to them; nothing from those summaries is used.
- **A green grass culm's modulus, shear strength and cutting energy:** no readable measurement. 1 GPa and 10 kJ/m² are assumptions inside the cereal and alfalfa bounds.
- **Straw's modulus** spans ten times between bending and tensile tests; 1.6 GPa is a derived midpoint, not a measurement. The downwash lean of tall straw turns on it.
- **The stem wall's density** for straw and dry culms (500 kg/m³) and green tissue (1000) are assumed.
- **Dry standing dead grass:** no field measurement. The weathering factors come from straw lying on the soil overwinter [7].
- **Burnt stems:** no source on charred herbaceous stems. The burnt_culm row is wholly assumed.
- **Weed stalks:** diameter is published for one species (open-grown); their wall, density, modulus and cutting energy come from alfalfa and woody-grass proxies; stalks per m² from one experiment's design densities, not a fallow survey.
- **The share of tillers standing as culms** at prop height (10–30 %) is assumed.
- **The prop's edge factor** (2–4) is bracketed, not measured. Nothing found measures cutting with an airfoil's round nose.
- **The critical-speed law** is derived here, checked against three published groups (grass culms, thick stalks, the support distance), and not taken from a source.
- **A leaf's petiole** cut energy (30 kJ/m²) has no source.
