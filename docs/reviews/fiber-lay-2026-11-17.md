# p7d-tether 1.11h, part one: the laid line in the pilot's 30 flights on model 103

QA's forensics of the pilot's flights since the model 103 build (OpenSpec `p7d-tether`, task 1.11h, the QA half). The pilot's words (D-117, rendered from Russian): a line laid over a roof "seems to have slid off somewhere" when they look back; when a prop winds up its own fiber "the break is instant"; laid round a house's corner, "the tension then goes from that corner on". Their account outranks any calculation (D-104). Nothing is fixed here: §7 lists the checks the physics engineer's fix must pass.

- **Tree:** `unity/qa/physics-integration-2--fiber-lay` at `86292fff`, the physics line at model 103, the model the pilot flew.
- **Flights:** the 30 logs the pilot wrote since the model 103 build, read in place. They are named here by an index of QA's own (1–30, in the order they were written) and their seed; nothing else about them is recorded. Flights 1–15: Calm, Sunny, 1 kg payload. Flights 16–30: Severe wind, Severe rain, 2 kg. All farmland, legs on.
- **The probe** (`fiber-lay-2026-11-17/probe/`): `lay.cs` replays a flight through `FlightCore` on its logged sticks from a scratch copy of the sim's `Runtime/` with read-only hooks (`hooks.patch`, `FiberLine.cs` only, under `#if HOOKS`: they report each trail freeze, lift and thaw with the terms that decided it). Every step's record is compared with the log's bit for bit, so every number below is the flight's own. **All 30 replay bit for bit with the hooks in** (§8). Nothing under `Runtime/` was changed. `triage.cs` reads the logs only (the player's summary lines, the events, the trail's count). `analyze.py`, `corners.py` and `summary.py` read the probe's output.
- **Data:** `fiber-lay-2026-11-17/stretches.tsv` (every stretch on a ruin, §2), `corners.txt` (every corner run, §3), `discs.tsv` (every pass through a disc, §4).
- **Timing:** none. The machine was shared; no time here is evidence (PLT-07).

## 0. Verdict

1. **Roof (the slide):** the sim did move laid line off ruins, but only in Severe wind. Of 173 stretches that lay pressed on a ruin, 10 left their place by more than 0.25 m, in 5 flights: 1 of 67 in Calm (the drone's crash dragged it) and 9 of 106 in Severe wind. The causes: the wash of the drone hovering 0.9–6.2 m away (6), the wind with the drone pulling from 14 m (2), the spool's pull (1) and the crash (1). They are physical in kind. On a roof or a wall's top the air is too strong: the sim reads both the wind and the rotors' wash there as free air over the terrain, because a roof is not a wall to the air (finding R1). Separately, in 13 flights the drawn line ran straight through ruin members for 49.6 s in all (finding R2). A pilot looking back sees that line pass through a wall, not over it.
2. **Corner:** the sim's corners do take the pull, as the pilot says. The line 1 m beyond stayed within 0.25 m in 131 of 148 wraps of 60° or more. But the run holds more than the wrap's capstan allows in 94 of them, 56 with a slack tail. Its run sits a median 0.45 m of line from the trail's frozen end. So the corner holds by the frozen trail behind it, not by Euler–Eytelwein (finding C1).
3. **Disc:** a turning prop whose blade reached the line cut it 0–2 ms after the line first entered its disc, in all 16 flights where a prop cut it. The summary named the prop and the tension each time. The disc held the line without a cut only in three cases: no blade reached it because the blades were broken to stubs (24 passes); the rotor had stopped (3); or a slow rotor's blades did not come round before the line left (2). No fault.

### 2.3 What holds a line there, by hand (derived unless labelled)
- **Weight** (tether.json through the probe's header line):
  - Sunny: 0.677 mN/m (d 0.25 mm).
  - Severe rain: 1.428 mN/m (d 0.40 mm, the 0.075 mm film).
- **Friction on a flat top.** The ruins' slots read μs 0.45, 0.60 and 0.65 (surfaces and materials through `FiberContacts`), so μs·w is:
  - 0.30–0.44 mN/m in Sunny;
  - 0.64–0.93 mN/m in Severe rain.
  - The trail's own hold is 0.34 and 0.71 mN/m (`span.ground_static_friction` 0.5, assumed).
- **The ruin's roof slope** (`Ruin_RoofCollapsed` 3933/3938, normal (−0.06, 0.81, −0.59), 36°):
  - Friction gives μs·cos 36° = 0.36 w at μs 0.45. Gravity pulls 0.59 w down the slope.
  - So line lying across that slope slides down by its own weight unless its tension or an edge holds it. That is right for the smooth boxes the ruin is built from.
- **The air's push across a line,** White's drag D = ½ρu²dC_d with C_d = 1 + 10 Re^(−2/3) (published; ρ 1.225 kg/m³, ν 1.5e-5 m²/s):

  | air across the line | Sunny line | Severe-rain line | Severe-rain line against its hold (0.64–0.93 mN/m) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 m/s | 0.39 mN/m | 0.65 mN/m | 0.7–1.0× |
  | 2.5 m/s | 1.9 mN/m | 2.5 mN/m | 2.6–3.8× |
  | 4.5 m/s | 4.7 mN/m | 7.0 mN/m | 7.5–11× |
  | 7.9 m/s | 12.6 mN/m | 19.6 mN/m | 21–31× |
  | 15 m/s | 42 mN/m | 65 mN/m | 70–100× |

  At the air the sim gave these lines (4.5–15 m/s), nothing a smooth face gives holds them: the moves in §2.2 follow from those airs.
- **What the air at a line lying on a roof should be.** The line's centre lies 0.2 mm off the face, inside the face's own boundary layer:
  - **The ground already has this.** The sim stills a lying line's wind inside the cover's layer (d + z0, `WindField`). It reads the wash at the line's own height in the wall jet's profile (design 20): 0.49–0.64 of the jet's peak at the crests.
  - **A roof gets neither.** In `FiberLine.Loads`, both `wind.At(x, _groundHeight[i])` and `_rotors.At(x, _groundHeight[i], …)` take the terrain's height under the node (`FiberLine.Ground`: `SampleGround` of the terrain), not the face the node lies on. So a node lying on a roof 6 m up reads the wind at 6 m above the terrain, and the wash as a free wake that has not yet met a wall.
  - **The difference, by the log law** (design 20's method): an 8 m/s wind at 10 m over a face of k_s 5 mm gives u_τ ≈ 0.30 m/s, and 8.5 u_τ ≈ 2.5 m/s at the face's crests. The sim gave 7.9 m/s. That is 3× in speed and about 8× in force.
  - **Even so, it moves.** 2.5 m/s still pushes 2.6–3.8× what holds a Severe-rain line on a smooth face, so an unsnagged line on a smooth roof would still creep in that wind. It would go less far and more slowly. The wash, read in the roof's own wall-jet profile, is about half as fast as now and a quarter of the force; its 15 m/s still moves the line.
- **The spool's pull.** At near-hover (the drone under 0.5 m/s), the exit's tension had a median of 0.1–5 mN in every flight but flight 1, whose median was 76 mN (90 %: 95 mN). The 90th percentiles reached 0.11 N in flights 11 and 27. That follows each flight's draw from the peel band (0.01–10 g, tether.json). A pull of 0.02 N needs about 28 m of Severe-rain line lying on a 0.71 mN/m hold, or a wrap, to hold it. A plank's 0.3 m of line holds about 0.2 mN. So flight 18's 0.29–0.37 m move under 0.016–0.027 N is that pull straightening the line, at its right size for the drawn tension.

### 2.4 The line drawn through the ruins (what the pilot may see without any slide)
- **How much.** In 13 flights a chain segment ran more than 5 cm inside a ruin's shape, for 49.6 snapshot-seconds in all. The worst: flight 24 for 26.3 s, flights 2 and 16 for 6.6 s each, flight 12 for 4.2 s. The deepest run was 1.55 m inside `Ruin_RoofCollapsed` shape 1861 (flight 20).
  - The probe tests each segment with a ray from each end. It then measures the chord's length inside the hit shape itself, every 5 mm against the shape's box, capsule, sphere or cylinder (`Through`, `Inside` in `lay.cs`).
  - **Frozen too:** 39 trail freezes in 7 flights laid a segment more than 5 cm into a ruin's shape (flight 24: 27; flight 12: 7; flights 2, 8, 13, 16 and 17: 1 each). These stay drawn until they are lifted.
- **One, traced** (flight 2, seed 0x6DABB60E19F4A12B, 31.1 s): node 16 (s 110.10 m) presses a timber of `Ruin_Walls_mirrored` at 1.33 m over the terrain. Its segment to node 17, 12.0 m away, runs straight through the wall's 0.24 m beam (shape 1732). The segment before it is 20.0 m.
- **Why.** The chain is full: 32 nodes for the whole live line, spaced 0.1 m at the exit and growing 1.5× a node beyond (design 3), so a ruin a few metres from the drone sits on segments of 1–20 m.
  - A member a segment meets takes a contact node only while the chain has room. The probe's ends count `FiberContacts.Refused` per flight. In the 19 flights that touched a ruin: 70–221 859 refusals (step-counts, median 13 635), against 23–2 037 nodes put.
  - Over the same flights the spacing law refused 848–155 738 splits.
  - So the segment lies through the member until room comes. Design 21 named this ("the chain's room was spent, so the board's contact node was refused … the spacing law's room against a ruin's many contacts is the cause to look at").
  - Here it is the drawn line: it goes through walls and beams, not over them.

### 2.5 The trail's end is given back on almost any pull (context, not a slide)
- **How often.** Over the 30 flights the trail's end was lifted 6 257 times: 5 262 `pulled-across`, 861 `pulled-corner` and 134 by the air (the probe's hook reports `Pulled`'s terms).
- **On how little.** `pulled-across` fired on an across pull of median 0.59 mN (90 %: 10 mN). The hold it beat was a median 0.036 mN: μs·w on the line the end lumps, a few centimetres of it.
- **So** the laid line near the drone is mostly live in the chain. That is where the wind and the wash act on it, and where the spacing law coarsens it. The lift moves nothing by itself: the chain's slots hold the point where it lay.
- **Physically,** a lying line pulled at an angle α by T bends over α·T/(μs·w) of its length, metres for these pulls, and moves centimetres. So the trigger is not wrong in kind; what the chain then does with the line decides.

## 3. The corners (task 2)
- **What was measured.** Every run of chain nodes on one ruin object with at least one pressing, at every snapshot, grouped into episodes (`corners.py`, `corners.txt`). For each run:
  - the wrap: the sum of the node turns;
  - the tension on the trail's side (T_far) and on the drone's side (T_near);
  - the capstan's bound e^(μs·wrap);
  - how far the line 1 m behind the run moved.
- **195 episodes, 148 of them wrapping 60° or more.** At each one's hardest pull, the drone's side carried a median 0.020 N (90 %: 0.154 N, at most 0.80 N).
- **The line beyond the corner** stayed within 0.25 m in 131 of the 148. In 17 it moved 0.26–1.53 m (`corners.txt`): one in Calm (flight 2, 0.26 m on `Ruin_Burnt`) and 16 in Severe wind (flights 16, 17, 20, 24, 26 and 29), mostly on `Ruin_RoofCollapsed` under the same wash and wind as §2.2.
- **The tension past the corner.** In 94 of the 148 the run held more than a wrap can: T_far < T_near/e^(μs·wrap), by more than 5 %.
  - **56 of them had a slack tail** (T_far under 1 mN) while the drone's side pulled a median 12 mN, up to 0.72 N. A wrap with a slack tail holds nothing (Euler–Eytelwein, published).
  - **The rest.** 54 sat within the bound, 19 of them with the trail's side the harder.
  - **The slide flag.** The run's nodes flagged a capstan slide in a median 85 % of an episode's snapshots.
- **What holds it.** The run sits a median 0.45 m of line from the chain's node 0, the trail's frozen end (0.31 m for the slack-tail ones; 88 of 148 within 2 m). The frozen end is anchored where it froze and holds whatever reaches it until `Pulled` lets it go.
  - So in the sim, "the tension goes from that corner on" because a frozen point sits just behind the corner. A wrap is not doing it.
  - How the run itself carries the excess over its bound within a step (its nodes' holds along the line while it is flagged sliding) is not traced here.
- **What a real wrap would pass** (published form, assumed μs):

  | wrap | μs 0.45 | μs 0.65 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 90° | 2.0× | 2.8× |
  | 180° | 4.1× | 7.7× |

  A tail lying on 20 m of ground gives a back tension of 6.8 mN in Sunny (μs·w·L). So a 180° wrap at μs 0.65 holds 52 mN. That is above the median pull measured and under its upper tenth. A real corner holds the pilot's pulls only with that back tension, or with more friction or wrap than a smooth box's arris gives.

## 4. The discs (task 3)
- **What was counted.** Every step, the probe ran the prop check's own geometry on every chain segment: inside a disc's slab, between hub and tip. It also counted whether a blade reached the crossing (r ≤ its tip × R) and whether one passed it in the step (`Discs` in `lay.cs`; `discs.tsv`).
- **45 passes through a disc, in 16 flights:**
  - **16 cut**, one per flight, each by a blade long enough to reach the line (longest blade 0.67–1.0 R), turning at 123–1 782 rad/s where the probe saw the entry. The cut came 0 ms after the first entry 6 times, 1 ms 6 times and 2 ms 4 times. So the line parted within the step it entered or the next two.
  - **For every cut the summary named the prop and the tension:** "cut by a prop in prop_rl's disc, at 0.0 N" (0.0–0.1 N).

## 6. What could not be decided
- **What the pilot looked at.** The logs can't say whether the pilot's "slid off" was one of the Severe-wind moves (§2.2, real in the sim) or the line drawn through a ruin (§2.4, in Calm too). Both are in their flights. In Calm, no stretch on a ruin moved except by the crash.
- **The air at a line on these roofs.** The size of the surface layer on the ruins' faces (rough masonry, broken slabs, timber) is not measured. 2.5 m/s is the log law's crest speed for k_s 5 mm (derived). A rougher face slows it, and a lee eddy over a ridge can reverse it. Whether an unsnagged real line on a ridge stays in an 8 m/s wind is the pilot's to say: it was not asked (D-024).
- **How a corner run carries more than its bound.** The run's nodes' holds along the line while it is flagged sliding were not traced to the solve's terms. And whether a real arris of masonry holds the pulls measured (median 0.020 N, 90 % 0.154 N) with a ground tail's back tension (6.8 mN for 20 m, Sunny) depends on μs on an arris, which is not measured.
- **The drawn picture.** The probe has no renderer. The claim that the pilot sees a chord through a wall rests on `FiberView` drawing straight tubes between the chain's nodes and the trail's points, read from its source, not looked at.
