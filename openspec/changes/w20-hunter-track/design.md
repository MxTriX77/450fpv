# Design: w20-hunter-track

## Context

See proposal.md for why. What the pilot's track file holds, surveyed read-only on 2026-11-21 (all **measured** from the file unless labelled):

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Objects | 334 rows of 30 kinds: 278 drawn, 56 data only (checkpoint volumes, spawn points, one player position, one repair trigger) |
| Doubled rows | 7 rows are byte-identical copies (5 concrete blocks, 1 pylon, 1 spawn point), leaving 327 distinct objects, 272 of them drawn. The race names none of the copies |
| Extent of the placed objects | x 400–562 m, z 396–545 m, about 162 × 149 m |
| Rotations | 66 of 329 posed objects need more than a yaw; ribbons hold their points separately |
| Scale | only on 39 objects, mostly the flexible checkpoint volumes |
| Race | 1 lap, 101 passages, 56 checkpoints, one linear path, 1,132 m between checkpoint positions in straight lines |
| Ground at standing objects | 47 samples at 19.91–20.27 m; best plane slope 0.013°, residual 60 mm SD, 295 mm at worst |
| Concrete blocks | 204 rows, 199 distinct; stacked in steps of about 0.76 m (**derived** from steps of 0.8, 1.5, 2.3, 3.0, 3.8, 4.6, 5.3 and 6.1 m); neighbours most often 0.8 or 1.5 m apart (**derived**, to be confirmed); the highest at 8.8 m above the ground |
| Low objects | 11 lie below 19.8 m, 6 of them frames at 15.7–19.2 m; 9 of the 11 have no standing object within 12 m |

The repository has two placement formats. The test range's holds a yaw only. The farmland's map package (`fpv450-map-package`) holds a full quaternion and a scale per axis, on height tiles, so this map uses the package. Both live on the build branch, not on the trunk.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** a flyable map after each portion; the file's poses unchanged; no change to the flight physics.

**Non-Goals:** see the proposal. In addition, no new generic track editor: the import serves this one file.

## Decisions

**1. Keep the file's coordinates.** The map rectangle is [0, 1000]² m like the farmland's, with the file's x, y and z unchanged. The layout already sits near its centre. *Alternative:* recentre at the origin (D-019), rejected because the farmland set the precedent and an unchanged frame makes the import auditable.

**2. The ground follows the objects, the objects do not move.** The terrain is a plane at the samples' fitted height plus a smooth local correction through the 32 distinct upright foot samples. *Alternative:* snap each object to a flat plane, rejected because stacks would have to move together and the poses would no longer be the file's.

**3. The 11 low objects are settled from clips X and Y, not guessed.** The file cannot say whether they stand in a dip or whether their origin is not at their foot. Task 1.1 decides per kind: an origin offset in the kit table, or extra height samples that make a dip in our ground. Either fits decision 2 and neither changes the tasks.

**4. Neutral kinds, matched without brand strings.** The import's kind table maps the file's ids by size and shape fragments to neutral kinds (`concrete_block`, `pylon_3m`, `frame_8x10`, and so on). The file's path is a command-line argument, so neither the path nor the source game's name is committed.

**5. One track-kit generator.** All kinds come from one parametric Blender generator with a params row per kind (AST-03), so portion 1's simple shapes and portion 2's models share ids, placements and physics metadata. Replacing a shape does not touch the layout. *Physics metadata per kind:* box or capsule collision following the visual, a surface from `physics_materials.json` (concrete, rubber, plastic, steel, fabric), gaps for gate openings, snag flags for ribbons and flags. Wind volumes for solid walls and stacks use the existing wake rules. Final parameters are recorded in `docs/object-sheets/hunter-track.md`, each labelled measured, derived or assumed.

**6. Sizes.** A name in the file gives some sizes directly (a 3 m pylon, a 2 × 4 m volume, a 300 × 220 cm gate). The frames' names give numbers without a unit, so clips X and Y settle them. Everything else is derived from spacing in the file or from the clips, and labelled.

**7. Branches.** `unity/world/w20-hunter-track` from the build branch. Finished portions merge there, and the trunk's build pointer is updated (D-122). The planning files land on the trunk.

**8. Doubled rows stand once.** The layout stays a faithful record of the file, all 334 rows. The map package places each byte-identical row once, because two coincident copies would double a collision shape and flicker on screen. *Alternative:* drop them in the import, rejected because the layout could then no longer be checked row by row against the file.

**9. The three houses (the pilot, 2026-11-21, D-124).** "Not simple as in the first houses from the demo map, but not the 100% copy either": our own parametric buildings, with the village map's house materials and the track's dimensions.
- *Dimensions:* footprint, storeys, wall heights and every opening come from the file's markers and clips X and Y (task 1.3b), each labelled. A marker is taken as the smallest its opening can be (**assumed**; markers are drawn inside their holes).
- *Materials:* the existing village house materials are referenced, and their textures stay in the local store (D-089). Nothing of the other game's model or texture is used.
- *Fit (**derived** from `drone.json`):* the drone is 0.574 m wide nose-first and 0.342 m tall with legs and 1 kg (0.304 m with legs off). 20 of the 23 markers pass it level. The windows are about 0.62 × 0.45 m, which leaves 23–28 mm each side and allows about 28–36° of pitch with legs on. Three are too small as marked: 416 (0.570 m wide), 394 (0.279 m tall) and 32 (0.330 m tall). The clips do not show them meaningfully larger, so 416 opens to 0.62 m, 394 to 0.40 m and 32 to 0.41 m (it shares one gap with marker 27, 0.406 m). They stay as hard as the rest.
- *The tree:* 4 of the 19 attached objects (a flag and three light rigs) hang on a tree 2.8 m past the north house's end. The file holds no tree, so the houses' generator adds a stand-in birch of our own with the batten that holds them.

**10. The start stands 1 m ahead of the file's spawn.** The file's spawn is on top of a concrete block lying on its side (**measured**: block top 20.404 m, spawn 20.399 m), the pilot's launch pad. A flight's start stands on the terrain, so at the spawn the drone begins inside the block, and its first step throws it out at 64 m/s and breaks the fiber (the pilot's first flight, 2026-11-22). 1 m along the heading clears the block by 0.79 m (**derived**). *Alternative:* start on the block's top, which needs a start with a height and a tilt in the session; left for after the pilot's notes.

**11. Full throttle under a house's floor (the pilot, 2026-11-25: "the quad grinds its props over the house floor, but it happens forever").** No law changes; the case is pinned by tests. What the evidence proves:
- *The logs.* Flights 30–39 never passed 0.66 of throttle stick. The only floor passage is flight 35's, 37.3–53.7 s, under house 274 at hover stick (0.41–0.45). There the crawl space is 0.41 m (**measured** from the log: ground 19.949 m, floor underside 20.360 m) for a drone 0.36 m tall with legs and the 2 kg bottle (**derived**, `drone.json`). The bottle strapped on top (D-050) stands 9.3 cm over the props' plane (**derived**). It touched the floor's underside 13 times. The props stayed 8.5–15 cm under it (the rotors' ceiling distance, **measured**), and no blade pass was logged. Flight 35 replays bit for bit to its last step on the build's world. The prop strikes on timber in flights 36 and 38 were on the end wall's window mullion and transom, 2.4 m up. They broke blades, jammed rotors and took a stalled motor's windings to 266 °C, a shorted coil.
- *The pilot's recipe, replayed.* Flight 35 was replayed to 38.0 s and then flown at full throttle. Level, the bottle is pinned to the floor with about 75 N and the props stay 9.3 cm under it. The motors run at full duty (160–180 A for the four). The windings warm until the first motor fails by heat at 104 s: its insulation's 220 °C rating shorts a coil, and the enamel's 300 °C breakdown fails it (Hobbywing's class wire; NEMA MW 1000's cut-through, `drone.json`, **published**). Then the drone falls and breaks on the ground. Leaned by a roll stick, it rolls about the bottle, and at 15–18° the low props reach the floor. Their passes carve the timber (softer than the blade: 12.5 against 165 MPa, **published**, Wood Handbook and Ertalon 66 GF30), jam within two passes and chip the tips to 0.85–0.88 R within 2 s.
- *So the props never ground the floor; the bottle did.* What full throttle does there is the motors' heat, over a minute and a half, which is the class's published rating (81 A for 9 s from 25 °C).
- *The headless case.* `FloorScenarios.UnderTheFloor` flies the pilot's flight's effects under a 0.06 m timber slab, T02's floor box. `FloorTests` holds it. With the 2 kg bottle, the props stay off the floor (no pass in 8 s, at least 50 mm clear) and the first motor fails by heat 86 s after full throttle, with no blade pass before. Pinned and then leaned, the props chip. Without the bottle (the known-wrong of the first case), the blades meet the floor, jam and break to stubs within seconds. `flightbench --floor` prints all three.
- *Found on the way* (model 108): a face lying within the blade's slab at the hub is met at radius 0, and `PropStrikes.Pass` dropped every meeting inside the blade root (model 64's rule against the vanishing lever). So a prop pressed flat into a face, within about 2° of its plane, made no pass at all: no work, no damage, no reaction and no motor load. On the rig, a disc 1 mm into timber at 0° and 2° made 0 passes in 2 s at full duty. The near-parallel bend force `k_f·p·cot β` was 0 there too. Fixed by the addendum below (the pilot, "Yes, fix it").

**11a. A turning prop pressed flat against a face (addendum, model change; `ContactSpec.FlatFaces`, header entry 36).** The blade meets a face it lies flat against by the same strike, jam, chip and break laws as a leaned one, with the contact derived from where the blade's slab is past the face. Nothing is special-cased at 0°: the angle sweeps continuously from flat to leaned.
- *The geometry (derived).* For a face past the tip (one that holds the blade's line beyond the tip; a shape inside the rim keeps model 64's root rule): sin β = |n·axis|, the in-plane slope sin γ = −n·ê along the meeting's direction ê, and d0, the slab's depth past the face at the hub along n: −r_m·sin γ while the hub's slab is clear of it, else the depth of the hub's own slab segment past it (`BladeCut.Meeting.Depth`, new). The slab is past the face beyond the chord x_m = −d0/sin γ (r_m; −∞ for a face parallel to the disc, clamped at the far rim). The tip is in the face over the arc 2α, cos α = x_m/r_tip (π, the whole turn, once the chord is past the far rim). The blade meets the face from r_in = max(x_m, root): its root, 0.1 R = 12.7 mm (drone.json's first station's inner edge), when the face reaches inside it.
- *Soft faces, the carve (derived).* At x the slab is past the face by d(x) = d0 + x·sin γ along n, so of the section's frontal height H the face holds clamp(d/sin β, 0, H) (the blade taken to its slab's face, as `BladeCut` meets it, assumed). Over the annulus from the root to the tip beyond x_m, the carved volume is ∫₀^H A(x_m + t·sin β/sin γ) dt and its polar moment likewise, in closed form (A and I a circle's segment's area and polar moment about its centre, their x-integrals Φ and Ψ, written in the depth u = x·sin γ so a parallel face is no special case). They scale model 108's H·A and H·I by fill and fillS: 1 for a face across the disc's plane (sin β 0, bit for bit model 108's carve), d0/(H·sin β) for a face parallel to it. Below a 0.1 µm wedge the midpoint's share is taken (numerical: the closed form's difference cancels there). A carve's work, fling, stones and the soil reaching the motor take the shares.
- *Hard faces, the bend (derived; corrects model 108).* To clear the face the tip must deflect along the axis by p/sin β, p the tip's depth past the face along n (d0 + r_tip·sin γ). Model 108's `k_f·p·cot β` is that law with the in-plane overshoot p/cos β written as the depth, so it fell to 0 for a face parallel to the disc. The bend's force is now `k_f·p/sin β`: 1.75 N for a tip 1 mm into concrete at every angle from 0 to 15°, against 0.03–0.47 N by model 108's. It changes a 20° wall chip's bend force by 1/cos 20°, +6 %. The crush is back to r_in, at its section.
- *The body's impulse (derived).* The face drags the blade along its motion all round the arc, so the body takes the mean of that direction: sin α/α of the drag's tangential impulse. This is exact for an even drag. It is 0 for a face parallel to the disc, whose drag turns with the blades, 1 − α²/6 for a graze, and 0.955 at α 30°.
- *Unchanged.* A break is searched inward of r_in as before, so a face reaching the root breaks no section by a carve's bending. That arc is then at least 84°, so a pass's force is at most the rotor's 93 J over 0.21 m at full speed (453 N), whose moment at the root station is 25 N·m against its edgewise 44 N·m (derived, drone.json's root section and σ_f). Its blades still break by the jam's grind and the held blade's plough, as a leaned carve's do.
- *The outcomes* (`FloorTests`, `flightbench --floor`). The 2 kg bottle's cases are unchanged: no blade pass in 8 s, the first motor failed by heat 86 s after full throttle, and the roll stick's lean chips the low props.
  - *Flown, without the bottle under T02's floor, 6 s from arming, full throttle from 1 s.* The motor bells stop the drone with the slab 1.4 mm into the timber when level (drone.json). At 0°, 1°, 2°, 5° and 15° the props meet the floor as the light drone's hover climbs into it, 0.52–0.62 s in. They jam within 0.1 s, and the held blades, dragged as the drone moves, break within 0.06 s of the first pass. By 6 s, 0°, 1°, 5° and 15° have lost a blade at its root; at 2°, a blade is chipped to 0.854 R and two stalled motors short at 3.0 and 3.6 s, one failing at 4.6 s.
  - *Clamped at full duty, the slab's deepest point 1 mm into timber (0° to 20°).* Every angle carves. The first pass's work falls from 693 J flat to 150 J at 0.5°, 67 J at 1°, 27 J at 2°, 7.3 J at 5° and 1.0 J at 20°. The rotor's speed over the last 0.5 s of the second rises from 0 (stalled) flat to 58 rad/s at 2°, 785 at 5°, 1265 at 15° and 1334 at 20°. Model 108 made 0 passes at 0° and 0.5°. At 2°, pressed from 3.8 to 4.6 mm through the root and the hub, the first pass's work grows from 542 to 805 J in steps under 6 %; model 108's fell from 2.3 kJ to nothing at the root.
  - *Clamped on concrete.* The blades bend over it and slide at every angle: 1.75 N, worn every pass, never jammed. Over the last 0.5 s of the second the rotor turns at 1346 rad/s flat and 1506 at 15°, against 1518 free.
- *Left as it is (suggested as separate changes).* A carved face's chord still immerses the disc past it (`Immerse`) at the meeting radius, so a face reaching the hub's slab takes half the disc's thrust however shallow it is. The held blade's plough in a grind takes the blade's full section past the chord, while only the slab's depth is in a flat face. Its impulse is held to what stops the blade's point, so it matters only for a fast slide.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The 11 low objects turn out to need a real dip] → Height samples from the clips, marked as estimates; the pilot checks them on the first flight.
- [Sizes estimated from clips are off by a few percent] → The pilot asked for no 100 % copy; every estimate is one params row to change.
- [Stacked blocks add many collision shapes] → 199 boxes are well below the map's existing object counts; the frame-rate and physics-step budgets are measured in portion 1.
- [The third vendor list touches the sim's loader] → House and Village goldens must pass unchanged before merge.
- [Cyrillic on the Play card] → If the menu font lacks those glyphs, task 1.5 adds them; the card keeps the pilot's spelling.
