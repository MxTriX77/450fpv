# Design: p7d-tether

## Context
- **What exists:**
  - The coil is a mass part (`drone.json` `coil`: a Ø0.083 × 0.20 m cylinder beneath the frame at y −0.053, its axis fore-aft, the line leaving aft through the rear cap's centre hole; 0.31 kg housing, 0.69 kg and 10 000 m of line). `DroneSpec.CoilMass(paidOut)` exists, but nothing pays out during a flight, and `AirframeModes` keeps the coil's mass constant ("the fiber's pay-out is 7d's").
  - `FeedSignals` carries `FiberTension`, `FiberMinBendRadius`, `LinkState` and `LinkMargin` as stubs.
  - Every conductor is a rigid capsule chain in `MapWorld` (the record's 0.5 m points at half its diameter; `WirePoints`, `WireParam`, `Geometry`'s `SpanA`, `SpanB` and `Sag`). 1.6b strikes it as a rigid shape: a prop on `L03_medium` at 9.51 m/s is a crash at 137 g, and a skid across it at 2 m/s takes 214.6 N on a leg and topples off.
  - The `wires` records carry diameter, mass per metre, sag, `axial_stiffness_n` (EA) and `breaking_force_n` (the pole-wires reading §2.2). The snag records carry `radius_m`, `edge_radius_m` and, on barbs, `barb_pitch_m` (the fortifications reading §1). No query serves snag records.
  - `DetMath` has √, cube root and `SinCos`; it has no exp, log or arctangent.
- **The map** (D-047): five `L03_medium` spans (4 bare A-50 conductors each, 40 m, 2 % sag) on six `L01` poles; S04's straw patches, V01's and V13's crowns, the Godot stand-in belt, H01's houses, E01's trench.
- **The pilot's words** (D-024: the account is the target, never a number to ask for): smooth orbits, never a sharp reverse, and a line that "may (or may not)" cut (VISION §2.5); it is cut by "overly sharp reverse, snagging on trees, very sharp lift off, or sudden momentum backwards after lift off" (PHY-19); wires "get tangled, twisted and flipped ... they are soft and a bit elastic" (D-059); thin wire wraps and tangles, thick cable deflects, stops or flips (PR-6).
- **The rule:** randomness only as a bounded perturbation of a named physical parameter from a named, logged stream; the ground, the world and the wires' geometry are never drawn.

## Goals / Non-Goals
**Goals:** PHY-18…22 physical, logged and replayable on the test range and on synthetic worlds; the pilot's accounts reproduced from causes, each statistical target failing a known-wrong model; determinism, 0 B and PLT-04 kept; D-054's judgement of the fiber's batch.
**Non-Goals:** the proposal's.

## Decisions

1. **The tether table** (`assets-src/tether.json`, physics-owned, the label rules of `physics_materials.json`; its hash in the header, a replay refusing another). Its blocks and starting values:

   - **Strength** (decision 7): the span's per-flight band, 0.69–1.4 GPa (floor published, the proof; ceiling assumed); the local section field, intact sections 3.0–5.0 GPa from a quantile table (assumed under the published 4.5–5.5 GPa short-gauge tensile and 6.5–7.6 GPa bending means), one flaw per 20 km of fiber (assumed) whose strength lies in 0.69–3.0 GPa, abraded sections 0.3–0.69 GPa (assumed: abraded glass, Weibull modulus 3–7, published); each band a piecewise-linear quantile table, so a draw is + − × ÷ only.
   - **Spool:** the pack's peel energy G₀ 0.02–0.10 J/m (published as the 2–10 g peel force of fiber canister packs, US 5,167,382 and its cited art, read at a 90° test angle), rising with the peel rate as G = G₀·max(1, (ṡ/v_ref)^n), n 0.2, v_ref 1 m/s (assumed from the published 0.1–0.3 exponents of rubbery adhesives' peel energy, Gent and Petrich 1969); a per-section irregularity of ±25 % (assumed: the pack's adhesive and cross-overs); the exit lip's radius 2 mm and μ 0.30 (assumed: a flared nozzle at the rear cap's Ø11 mm hole, the drone sheet; GetFPV's canisters use an aluminium outlet nozzle, published); the exit point at the rear cap's centre, (0, −0.053, −0.100) m in the body (derived, `drone.json`).
   - **Link:** 1550 nm (assumed: the video downlink of a bidirectional pair); the budget between launch power and receiver sensitivity 15 dB (assumed from the published 20 km BiDi SFP class: −8…−3 dBm out, −23 dBm sensitivity), less 1.5 dB of connectors and splices (assumed); attenuation 0.21 dB/km (published typical of G.657 at 1550, under ITU's 0.30 maximum); macrobend loss per full turn L(R) = 0.5 dB·exp(−0.644·(R − 7.5 mm)/mm), fitted through ITU-T G.657.A2's published limits (0.5 dB at 7.5 mm, 0.1 dB at 10 mm, one turn, 1550 nm), so 2.5 dB at 5 mm, 9.1 at 3 mm and 17.2 at 2 mm (derived; limits are maxima, so this is the worst compliant fiber, assumed); microbend loss at a loaded contact c_m·q·ℓ_c with c_m per surface class (assumed); the receiver's threshold at 0 dB margin with 1 dB of hysteresis (assumed).
   - **Wires' fittings:** a bare conductor's tie on a hook or pin holds 0.5–2.0 kN before slipping and leaves its seat after 0.2 m of slip (assumed: of the order of 10–25 % of the conductor's rated strength); a SIP anchor clamp holds its conductor's rated strength (assumed); a service drop's house anchor pulls out at 1–3 kN (assumed); the conductors' rated strength drawn per conductor in 0.9–1.2 × the record's (assumed: the ГОСТ figure is a minimum, old conductors corrode).
   - **The launch:** the line's far end anchored at the ground station 3 m behind the start point on the ground (assumed), laid straight on the ground to beneath the exit at the start (assumed: the pilot sets the drone down on its line).

2. **One cable solver** (`Runtime/Tether/CableChain`). The fiber's and a conductor's stiffness are far beyond what an explicit 1 kHz step carries: a fiber segment of 0.1 m has ω·dt ≈ 36 axially and 5 transversely at the proof tension; a 0.5 m A-50 segment ≈ 9.6 axially (derived). So each chain is stepped by **linearly implicit (backward) Euler inside the same 1 ms step**: per segment the tension-only spring EA/ℓ₀ along its axis and the geometric stiffness T/ℓ across it, the contact and anchor springs on each node's diagonal, one block-tridiagonal solve (Thomas, no pivoting; 3 × 3 blocks inverted by cofactors; + − × ÷ only), then positions from the new velocities. The drone keeps its semi-implicit Euler; a chain's forces on the drone are the chain's end-of-step forces, applied to the drone's next step (a one-step lag, its energy bounded in decision 13).
   - **Why not explicit with smaller segments:** the mass falls with ℓ while EA/ℓ rises, so it gets worse; sub-stepping is forbidden (PHB-01).
   - **The price, labelled:** backward Euler damps a mode by about ω·dt/2 per radian: under 0.2 % for a conductor's 0.6 Hz sway, about 5 % at 100 rad/s, more for the axial modes, which is what keeps a tension spike from ringing into a spurious cut (derived). It never adds energy.
   - **Bending stiffness** is not a chain degree of freedom: a fiber's or a conductor's EI acts where the line bends over something, through the elastica's radius (decision 6, decision 10).
   - **Sliding contact nodes** (an arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian node): every node carries its material coordinate s (unstretched metres from the line's fixed end); a free node keeps its s; a node on a contact (an edge, a twig, a drone part, the exit lip, a prop hub) may let material slide through it. Each step, with T₁ and T₂ the two segments' tensions and θ the wrap there: if max/min ≤ e^(μ_s·θ) it holds, else it slides until max/min = e^(μ_k·θ) (Euler–Eytelwein's capstan, published), a closed-form shift of s because T is linear in ℓ₀ (derived).
   - **Insertion and removal** by state alone: a segment longer than its target is split at its midpoint (position and velocity linear, mass and rest length halved, momentum kept); two adjacent segments shorter than their targets merge (momentum kept, kinetic energy can only fall). Canonical order: from the line's fixed end.
   - **As built (1.5): never gaining energy in one linear step.** Every force enters the solve as a quadratic model equal to its true energy at the step's start, so backward Euler on the models dissipates; where the true energy is not quadratic the model is chosen above it: a taut segment's exact tangent (EA/ℓ₀ along, T/ℓ across); a slack segment predicted to tighten within the step a spring along its direction from its start length; a node on or in a member the full spring from the surface; one predicted to reach it a spring from its start gap (a majorizer; the node can stop up to its approach a step short of the surface, then closes). The prediction is explicit, so a touch or a tightening it misses (the line's pull reaches a node through the chain within the step) is taken speculative and the step solved again from its start, at most twice (an active-set correction of the same dt). What the models still under-estimate at the end (a taut segment stretched sideways, ½k·ε⁴/(8ℓ²) to leading order) is their deficit, computed exactly: a rise within it is taken back from the line's motion about its mean velocity (momentum kept; labelled numerical damping); a rise the motion cannot give back solves the step once more on majorizing models (a segment's spring isotropic, k·I about the same force; every member speculative), which cannot gain energy and is over-stiff across the line for that one step. A rise after that, or a non-finite value, is a `TetherWedge`: the step undone, the line stopped. A capstan run (neighbouring nodes on capstan members) is one wrap; its slip sets the profile e^(μk·θ) node by node from the low side, the run's material kept (a convex 1-D Newton solve for the low tension), the moved mass pooled at its mean velocity (momentum kept); a slide or a merge that would raise the energy waits a step. A capstan node's hold is its member's point (the caller places it); a surface's hold slips by Coulomb.
   - **Used by:** the fiber (decision 3), each active conductor (decision 9).

3. **The spool, the span and the laid trail** (PHY-18, DRN-05).
   - **The exit** is a contact node of the drone at the rear cap's centre. The line inside the canister leaves the pack over its end (stationary pack, over-end payout, the drone sheet's reading). **Payout is one-way:** material flows out (s at the exit grows) only while the tension at the exit, less its capstan over the lip, exceeds the pack's resistance T_r = G(ṡ)·(irregularity) + μ_l·ṡ² + μ_l·L_b·s̈ (the peel, the momentum flux and the balloon's line of L_b 0.25 m, assumed; the FOG-M review's form: a friction-and-adhesive term plus the line's ρv², published); it never flows back. `payout_tension_n` is T_r, named and logged every step.
   - **The lip:** the angle θ_e between the canister's aft axis and the first segment is the lip's wrap; the tension beyond the lip is T_r·e^(μ·θ_e) (published capstan; 1.17× at 30°, 1.60× at 90°, 2.57× at 180°, derived).
   - **What it gives** (derived, checked in 1.5): the line pulls the drone at its exit with at most 0.02–0.1 N at rest and about 0.06–0.2 N at 20 m/s cruise with the line trailing (the peel at 1.8 × G₀, plus 0.028 N of momentum flux); far below the weight, but below and behind the centre of mass, so it is a small, real pitch and roll moment the loop feels, growing whenever the line is held.
   - **The span:** at most 32 active nodes from the exit to the touchdown, spaced 0.1 m within 1 m of the exit (the props' scale) and growing by 1.5× a node beyond (assumed: 32 nodes cover about 1.5 km), each a sliding contact node when on a contact.
   - **The laid trail:** a node that has rested on the ground or an object's top for 0.5 s (speed under 5 mm/s, assumed), held by its friction against its segment's tension, is frozen into the trail (its position, s and contact identity), up to 16 384 points (assumed: 10 km at 0.6 m; overflow merges the oldest pair and logs a `WorldBug`). When the tension at the touchdown exceeds the trail's holding friction (μ times the weight of the next 1 m), trail points unfreeze back into the chain, last frozen first. The world stores no line; the trail is flight state.
   - **Loads on each node:** gravity; drag on the relative wind from the wind field (`p7c-wind`) by decision 1's C_d (a falling line settles at 1.4 m/s at Re 24, derived: from 50 m up the line takes about 35 s to land, so a cruise trails a long airborne arc); each rotor's inflow and wash (decision 4); rain's wet film (decision 5).
   - **The coil's mass:** the mass model is rebuilt each 10 m of payout (0.69 g, assumed step), the angular momentum kept; `AirframeModes`' coil mass follows. The leaving line carries the drone's momentum away, so the mass change itself is no force (derived); its reaction is the momentum flux already in T_r.

4. **The rotors' air on the line.** A light line near the props is moved by their air long before it is struck: the hover's induced velocity is 10.1 m/s (5.18 kg, derived), whose drag on the fiber is about 31 times its weight (derived).
   - **Above a disc,** inside its radius: the vortex cylinder's on-axis inflow w(z) = v_i·(1 − z/√(z² + R²)) toward the disc (published, Johnson 1980, Leishman 2006); outside its radius: a hemispherical sink of the disc's flux πR²·v_i, speed Q/(2πd²) toward the hub (derived).
   - **Below a disc:** the wake at 2v_i within R/√2 (published far-wake contraction), turning into a radial wall jet when the disc is within 2R of the ground, u = Q/(2πr·h_j), h_j 0.1R (derived from mass conservation, h_j assumed).
   - **So:** the jet lifts a line lying under a disc at liftoff, and the sink draws a line near a disc's top or edge into it. v_i per rotor comes from its own thrust (the propulsion model), so a full-throttle punch moves the line hardest.
   - **The fountain and the ground vortex** (1.6b, D-075: the path by which a line under the rear reaches a disc). Where two rotors' wall jets meet (at a point, the two fastest; the line of equal speed, the midpoint of equal rotors as Lee et al. 2026 measured), their normal flux and momentum rise as a Gaussian plane jet (spread 0.10, published) from its virtual origin, 0.711 of the jets' momentum (derived from Dekker et al. 2023's fountain reaching 3R at H/R 4), from the ground's stagnation line up to the discs' plane, where their intake takes it; whole up to μ* 0.4, gone by 0.9. A wake landing in a stream along the ground rolls a ground vortex ahead of it: a straight Scully vortex with its image, its centre (1.0175 R + 0.2707 r_p) ahead of the landing, r_p where the wall jet meets the stream, its circulation 1.185 v_h·R (both derived from Wolf et al. 2022's flight test at μ* 0.55 and 0.80), growing to μ* 0.4 and gone by 1.19; a rotor whose upstream jet meets another's landing forms none. Design 4's field alone stays as the known-wrong (`FlightSetup.TetherDesign4Air`).

5. **Rain** (D-021: "a heavier, draggier fiber span"). In Rain the line carries a water film of 0.05 mm (assumed), so 0.35 mm across and +68 % of its mass (derived); in Severe rain 0.075 mm (assumed). The falling drops' momentum on the line is 10⁻⁵ of its weight and left out (derived). The rain level is `p7c-wind`'s.

6. **The fiber's contacts.**
   - **The ground:** a node within 0.5 m of the ground samples it (`SampleGround`, one batch); it rests on the soil, or on a straw mat's top (the straw box's top face, which the line does not sink into: assumed), with the surface's polymer μ; anchored elements that lie over the line hook it by p7a's over-side rule and release force.
   - **Shapes:** each active segment is a swept capsule of the line's radius against `StaticContacts` (the batch rule, decision 12); rigid rows give their face and `edge_radius_m`; the homogenised `twig`, `foliage` and `razor_wire` shells are skipped (the fortifications reading N-3): the line reads their elements and records instead.
   - **Elements** (p7a's `MicroDetailNear`): a twig or a crown's twig is a capstan of its own radius, a standing stem a cantilever the line drags through (p7a's law), a lying straw a hook.
   - **Snag records** (`SnagsNear`): each segment of a record is a capstan of its `edge_radius_m` (else its `radius_m`); with a `barb_pitch_m` the contact point is locked along the record within half a pitch of where it touched, while the line may still slip along its own length (the fortifications reading §2.5).
   - **Wires:** a conductor (static or active, decision 9) is a capstan of its strand's `edge_radius_m`.
   - **The drone's own parts and the stand:** the legs, skids, arms, cans, packs, the coil and the stand's bars and posts are contacts like any shape, and the line's reaction acts on the drone (or on p7a's free stand) at the contact point.
   - **The wedge** (PHY-18): a node touching two members takes both contacts together on its diagonal; nothing in the implicit step can then gain energy (decision 13).
   - **Every bend is known:** at each contact the bend's radius is R_eff = max(R_edge + c, λ/(2·sin(θ/4))), λ = √(EI/T), with θ the line's turn there (the elastica over a point edge, the fortifications reading §2.1; published planar elastica, derived form); the local stress is σ = E·r/R_eff + T/A_g. The minimum R_eff over the line is `FiberMinBendRadius`.

7. **Cut risk, never certainty** (PHY-19). The line breaks when any of these holds, checked every step, the first in this order logged as the mechanism:
   - **prop:** an active segment crosses a turning disc between its hub and its tip (from the rotor angle `p7a-ground` 1.15 adds; the line is struck at the next blade pass): a 0.25 mm glass line struck at 50–120 m/s is cut (PHY-19: "a line–prop contact is a cut", assumed certain);
   - **tension:** σ_t = T/A_g anywhere on the active span reaches the span's per-flight strength σ_span (stream `fiber.span_strength`, one draw at arming: 8.5–17 N);
   - **bend:** at a bend (a contact, the exit lip, the peel point) σ reaches the local strength of the fiber section there (stream `fiber.section_strength`: one draw per 0.1 m section, indexed by s, so the order of use never shifts a draw; logged with `FiberSection` the first time it is read);
   - **abrasion:** a section that rubs at a contact wears its coating by Archard's law, h += k·p·Δx/H (published form; p the Hertz pressure of the line on the edge, Δx the slide); worn through, its local strength is redrawn from the abraded band (the same section's second draw), and the next bend there is checked against it;
   - **the peel point:** the line bends at the pack's peel front with κ = √(2G/EI) (published, the elastica at a peel front: bending energy equals the adhesion energy), 1.0–2.2 GPa at G₀ (derived); a sharp liftoff's peel rate raises G, and with it the stress, on the sections leaving at that moment.
   - **Why two strengths:** fiber strength depends on the stressed length (Weibull's weakest link, published): a whole span in tension breaks near its proof-truncated low tail, while a few millimetres bent at a contact are far stronger unless a rare flaw or an abraded spot sits there. One per-flight number for both would either cut every span at the peel or never at a bend.
   - **So "may or may not"** has three physical sources, none of them a roll on the outcome: where the slack goes (the wind's turbulence, the trail's lie, the rotors' air), which section is at the bend, and the span's strength.
   - **What the pilot's causes become:**
     - **a sharp reverse:** the spool cannot take line back, so the slack lies ahead of the drone as it comes back, inside the discs' sink, and the lip's wrap grows toward 180° (2.2 GPa and 8 dB at 0.2 N over the 2 mm lip, derived);
     - **snagging on trees:** a crown's twigs and limbs hold the line by capstan; the drone flying on raises nothing (the spool pays out) until the line wraps a part of the drone or the wrap at the snag grows, and then the snag's bend and abrasion and the line's own tension do the cutting;
     - **a very sharp liftoff:** the peel rate's stress at the pack, the wall jet lifting the line laid under the drone into the sink, the line held under a skid, a post or a hook as the drone leaps;
     - **backward momentum after liftoff:** the drone moves back over the line rising from the launch point into its rear discs, or catches it on a leg or the stand.
   - **After a break:** the drone's end of the line stays on the spool side (its piece hangs from the exit and still drags, still cuttable but harmless); the far piece stays where it lies.

8. **The link margin and the break** (PHY-20, PHY-21, PHY-25).
   - **The margin** each step: 13.5 dB − 0.21 dB/km × the paid-out length − Σ over bends L(R_eff)·θ/2π − Σ over loaded contacts c_m·q·ℓ_c (decision 1). A line hanging free loses nothing to bends; a snag, the exit lip at a sharp angle, a wire or a barb takes several dB, and a line fluttering at a snag in the wind makes the bend and the loss move with it: "vibration" enters only that way. The margin never reads the attitude rate or rain on the lens (K§8.7).
   - **A dip** is the margin crossing 0 dB; it ends when it is back above 1 dB (hysteresis); `LinkDip` logs its start, end, minimum and the bend or contact with the largest loss.
   - **The break:** `LinkState` becomes Broken in the step of the break and the margin falls to −∞ in that step, so the feed's machine sees a loss within a field (its hard-cut or staged branch, K§11.3); `TetherBreak` logs the mechanism, the place (the contact's identity: object and shape, element `Id`, snag record, wire, drone part, the lip or the peel), s, T, θ, R_eff, σ and the strength it met.
   - **The failsafe** (D-023, PHY-21): the uplink is the same line (assumed: jamming immunity is why the class flies fiber), so a break is the receiver's signal loss. The flight controller runs Betaflight 4.5's stages with their CLI names: the hold until signal loss is declared, stage 1's channel fallbacks for `failsafe_delay`, then stage 2's `failsafe_procedure` (Drop disarms; Land holds `failsafe_throttle`, the pilot's 1400, for `failsafe_off_delay`, then disarms); values pinned in 1.7 from Betaflight 4.5's published defaults and the pilot's profile. `Failsafe` logs each stage.
   - **The feed's signals live** (PHY-25): tension at the exit, the minimum bend radius, the link state and the margin, as 50 Hz field means and maxima.

9. **Soft wires: the swing and the active chain** (PHY-22, D-059).
   - **Every conductor swings** while the drone is far: its rest catenary (the record's points) turns about its chord by the angle θ of one first-mode pendulum, θ̈ = −ω₁²·(θ − atan(q/w)) − 2ζω₁·θ̇, with ω₁ = 2π·√(T/μ)/(2L) (published, a taut string's first mode: 0.62 Hz for A-50 at 40 m, derived), q the drag per metre from the wind field at mid-span (decision 1's C_d), w the weight per metre, ζ 0.01 (assumed structural; the aerodynamic part is in q). In 10 m/s across a span this is the lines sheet's 20–40° blow-out (derived). Twenty conductors cost under a microsecond.
   - **Activation:** when the drone's bounds or an active fiber segment come within 2 m (assumed) of a conductor's swung shape, it becomes a chain (decision 2) on its record's points from that shape and the swing's velocity, with its record's mass per metre and EA, and a rest length per segment so that its static tension is the record's T = w·L²/(8f) (derived). At most 8 conductors are active (assumed: a line's four and a neighbour's; overflow is a `WorldBug`). A chain returns to the swing when every node is within 5 mm of the swung shape for 1 s with no contact and the conductor intact (assumed); a broken or dropped conductor stays a chain, and once it rests on the ground or on objects it freezes like the fiber's trail and stays a snag for the line, the legs and the props.
   - **Refinement near the drone:** segments within 1 m of the drone are split to 0.05 m (assumed: an arm's edge, a leg or a can), merged back beyond 2 m; the record's points are never moved, only refined between.
   - **What it gives** (checked as scenarios, derived from the published theory, never set): a sideways push at a point x along a span meets T·L/(x(L − x)) (a taut string: 33.1 N/m at A-50's mid-span, 174 N/m 2 m from a pole); pressed down in its own plane it meets Irvine's linear-theory stiffness, k = (H/L)/(1/4 − (3/16)/(1 + 12/λ²)) with λ² = (wL/H)²·L/(H·L_e/EA) (published, Irvine 1981): λ² 240 and 116 N/m at A-50's mid-span (derived); past its 42.7 mm of slack it stretches through EA until it breaks at T_b, which a rigidly held A-50 span does after 431 J (derived, the pole-wires reading §2.2); a strike's first force is the wire's impedance 2√(Tμ) times the speed, 13.4 N·s/m on A-50 (published for a string).
   - **The world's wire stays the rest shape** in the content hash; physics owns the live state, and `StaticContacts` and `Raycast` skip wires for physics' calls (world-builder, decision 12), so the drone's and the line's contacts with wires come only from physics' set, and the rotor rays never read a wire as a ceiling (the pole-wires reading N-4).

10. **Wires on the drone's parts.**
    - **Contact:** each active segment, a capsule of the conductor's radius, against the drone's contact shapes (1.6's 43: arm edges, cans, the stack, packs, the coil, the payload, legs, skids); the part's stiffness in series with the wire as every body pair (P7), the wire node's side implicit, the drone's side as now.
    - **Sliding:** a stick anchor along the part and along the wire with the `cable` row's μ 0.30/0.25 (assumed, the row); past it the contact point moves along both, so a conductor caught under an arm slides out to the can and off, or into the arm's root.
    - **Wrapping** is the chain conforming round a part by its refined nodes, never tighter than its own elastica allows: R ≥ max(the part's radius + D/2, λ_w/(2·sin(θ/4))), λ_w = √(EI/T) with EI from the record (decision 15); a conductor of 7–16 cm bending length (the pole-wires reading §2.3) stands off a 6 mm arm edge and touches it at a few points, a slack thin wire wraps it.
    - **Entangled:** a conductor on two or more parts carrying more than half the drone's weight for 0.5 s (assumed) is logged `WireEntangled`; the drone hangs, swings and turns on it by the contacts' moments. A contact above the centre of mass trips the drone over the wire (a pitch or roll flip); one at the legs swings it under.

11. **Prop strikes on wires and winding** (PHY-22's thin wire; PHY-26's seam).
    - **The pass:** a conductor segment inside a turning disc is struck at each blade pass (the rotor angle). The blade at radius r_s and speed v = ω·r_s must give the wire's chord-length of mass and its impedance over the pass the impulse P = c·(μ·v + 2√(Tμ)), c the blade's chord 0.025 m (assumed). If P·r_s ≥ J·ω (the rotor's angular momentum, J from `drone.json`), the blade cannot clear it: it stops on the wire (`RotorJammed`, the wire caught). Otherwise the rotor loses P·r_s of angular momentum (its speed drops, the motor model recovers it) and the wire takes P along the blade's motion (derived from impulse–momentum).
    - **What it gives** (derived at 9000 rpm, J 6.0e-5 kg·m²): on a taut A-50 (331 N) a blade 50 mm out clears it losing 44 % of its speed, 90 mm out it is on the edge, at the tip it stops; on slack wire every radius clears, flinging the wire round with the blade.
    - **Winding:** a caught or flung wire whose segment comes round the hub is held at the hub (a sliding contact node at the hub's radius, 7.5 mm, assumed): the motor turns the hub against T·r_hub (its load torque added to the rotor's equation), drawing the wire's material onto the hub. An intact span takes 4.2–5.7 N·m to wind against 0.38–0.51 N·m of motor torque (the pole-wires reading §2.3), so it stalls within its slack; slack wire (a span already broken or dropped, or pulled slack by the drone's own push) winds turn after turn until its tension stalls the motor. `WireWound` logs the rotor, the turns and the length.
    - **The seam with `p7f-damage`:** each pass writes the strike to `FlightState.BladeStrikes` (energy, impulse, kind `wire`, the conductor's diameter and material) as p7a's vegetation strikes do; 7f decides what the blade suffers.

17. **The hole's edge in the pilot's four causes** (p7d 1.11; the physics ledger's gap 4, P35–P38; worked on model 85's line, 2026-11-13).     - **The edge's stress is bounded by the pack** (derived). The lip sits between the pack and everything outside it, so its outer tension is at most the one-way spool's law, T_lip ≤ e^(μ_l·θ)·(G·irr + m_b·s̈) + μ·ṡ² (decision 3), whatever holds the line beyond it (a crown, the drone's parts, the ground): a hold outside raises only the tension outside. Over a point edge the glass bends at the elastica, σ = 2·sin(θ/4)·E·r·√(T/EI) + T/A_g (decision 6). The tension that reaches the intact band's 3.0 GPa floor, and the G·irr that gives it through the lip's capstan: 90° 0.62 N (0.39 N); 120° 0.37 (0.20); 135° 0.30 (0.15); 150° 0.25 (0.115); 165° 0.22 (0.091); 180° 0.19 (0.073). The band's top is G·irr 0.123 N: at the published peel the edge tears an intact section only past about 150° of wrap and on the upper part of the band (σ 3.1 GPa at 150°, 3.9 at 180° at the top; 2.2 and 2.7 at the band's middle). Otherwise only a flawed section (0.69–3.0 GPa, one per 20 km, assumed) or one worn through at the edge breaks there.
    - **The snap when slack is taken up** (derived). The line's axial impedance is Z = √(EA·μ) = 0.249 N·s/m (wave speed √(EA/μ) = 3603 m/s). A line jerked taut against the spool meets the balloon's 17 mg (μ·L_b) through the lip: the junction's peak is Z·Δṡ·k/(1 + k), k = e^(μ_l·θ), 0.171–0.179 N per m/s at 150–180°, lasting m_b·k/Z = 0.15–0.18 ms before the payout catches up; a 1 kHz step carries it as k·m_b·Δṡ/dt, about a quarter of the peak. It would matter only for a take-up of metres per second within a step. A sagging line is not one: the transverse acceleration its vanishing sag d demands raises its tension as 1/d⁴, so it reaches the spool's hold with sag left (0.26 m over a 3.7 m span at 2.8 m/s, derived), and from there the payout tracks the drone. Measured (`FiberCutTests.ProbeTheLipInTheCauses`, seed 1, the session's drone): the largest change of ṡ in one step is 0.12 m/s in the sharp liftoff, 0.10 in the backward move after liftoff, 0.19 and 0.30 in the sharp reverses from 54 and 90 km/h; the only large one, 6.23 m/s, is a pitch-back at the cruise's throttle from 54 km/h that sinks 10 m into the floor.     - **Twist and hockles** (published, derived). Over-end payout twists the line a turn per turn of pack (its radius r_p 20–40 mm), so the line carries a moment M = G·J/r_p of 1.9–3.7e-5 N·m (G = E/2(1+ν), J = 2I) and hockles when slack below M²/(4EI) = 0.1–0.4 mN (Greenhill's limit). A hockle forms at radius EI/(2M) and, pulled, tightens only to EI/M, 23–47 mm, where it snaps open (the energy balance in arXiv 2108.00271, "Writhing and hockling instabilities in twisted elastic fibers"; Coyne 1990, IEEE J. Oceanic Eng. 15, on the loop's pop-out): 0.1–0.2 GPa of bend. A twisted line's loop does not kink at the edge.
    - **A blade's strike** (derived): a blade at 137–157 m/s strains the struck line 0.9 % by Smith's transverse impact (0.66 GPa), on top of the bend over the blade's 0.5 mm leading edge (9.1 GPa): a line in a turning disc is cut, as decision 7 assumes.

18. **The fiber at the exit, corrected from the sources, and the two liftoff causes** (p7d 1.11, the physics ledger's gap 4; continues decision 17 on model 88's line, 2026-11-13; the sources are [fiber-exit.md](../../../docs/reference-notes/fiber-exit.md), cited there by number). Labels as everywhere: published, derived, assumed.
    - **The lip's law.** The capstan law T₂ = T₁·e^(μθ) holds for a line that conforms to the surface. The exit's edge (`spool.lip_radius_m` 0.3 mm, assumed) plus the coating (62.5 µm) is far under the line's own radius of curvature at the exit's tensions (1/κ = 1.7 mm and more at 0.27 N and 150°, derived), so the line touches it at one point and turns through two free elastica arms, φ₁ on the pack's side under T₁ and φ₂ outside under T₂, φ₁ + φ₂ = θ (fiber-exit.md §2.3, derived; Singh 2022 [4] the published check: always under the classical ratio). The contact's two conditions, curvature continuous, √T₁·sin(φ₁/2) = √T₂·sin(φ₂/2), and the tangent's balance sliding outward, T₂·cos φ₂ − T₁·cos φ₁ = μ·(T₁·sin φ₁ + T₂·sin φ₂), give T₂/T₁ = sin²(φ₁/2)/sin²(φ₂/2) as a function of θ and μ alone (the tension cancels), solved by bisection on φ₁ in (θ/2, θ) (a fixed 60 halvings, DetMath, 0 B). The glass's stress at the edge is the outer arm's, σ = E·r·2·sin(φ₂/2)·√(T₂/EI) + T₂/A_g, so R_eff = 1/κ. **Where the line really wraps** (R_edge + c ≥ 1/κ under the point law: the flared 4 mm known-wrong, never the 0.3 mm edge under 5 N), the capstan law and decision 6's bend stay. The choice for the spool's factor is made on the last step's exit tension (a one-step lag, as the factor is set before the step); for the stress, on this step's.
    - **The second contact (fiber-exit.md §6's open point), in the sim's own geometry.** The nozzle is a Ø18 mm tube with a Ø12 mm bore, 70 mm long (`spool.mouth_radius_m`, `spool.nozzle_length_m`). (1) **Inside, the bore.** A line pulled off the axis lies across the bore before it reaches the edge, so its inner arm leaves along the bore's diagonal, up to the free angle α = atan(2·r_m/L_n) = 9.7° (derived; already the wear's), and the turn at the edge is θ − α; the entry's own turn, min(θ, α), is a second point contact in series, at a stress of 0.13 GPa at 0.1 N (derived), so it enters the ratio only. The spool's factor is then k(min(θ, α))·k(max(0, θ − α)): continuous, 1 at θ = 0. (The 12.5° bend at the collar, on the line's way from the pack into the nozzle, is not modelled before or after: it is the same at every θ.) (2) **Outside, the tube's outer arris.** Past θ ≈ 90° the line leaves over the 3 mm end face and bears on the outer arris too (two-point contact, Singh's case [4]). Solved by shooting the planar elastica through both arrises (the scratchpad's `lip.py`, derived): at 120–165° and μ 0.45–1.0, 0.03–0.3 N, the two-point solution's largest stress is 10–40 % under the single point's (150°, μ 0.45, T₁ 0.1 N: 1.62 against 2.26 GPa) and its tension ratio 2–40 % over it (2.61 against 2.33); two independent point contacts overstate the ratio (2.92) and understate the stress (1.38 GPa). The two-point case is not built: the single point at θ − α bounds the edge's stress from above, so every conclusion below about whether an intact section tears holds a fortiori; it understates the tension outside past 90°, which the drone feels as at most 0.05 N more at the exit (derived at the band's top). It is the next correction if a target ever turns on the outer tension.
    - **μ_l 0.45, derived**: the median of the 11 published values of a cured acrylate coating film on stainless steel, band 0.36–1.0 (published [1]); the coating's own shear strength sets μ on a hard smooth counterface, so steel stands in for the aluminium nozzle (assumed); Amontons' law (constant μ: the contact is near fully plastic, §2.2). Not drawn per flight: the coating is one product's, and the band is printed as the harness's sensitivity (0.36 and 1.0).
    - **The peel band widened downward** (published [9]): G₀ as a quantile table, 0.01–2 g (the reduced-adhesive pack, which "pays out satisfactorily") on half the flights and 2–10 g on the other half (the split assumed: the patent gives the two bands as alternatives, the drone sellers write "near zero"), read at the 90° test angle as before (G = F).
    - **The edge's wear at a point contact.** The line now presses the edge with N = T₁·sin φ₁ + T₂·sin φ₂ at one point: Hertz of crossed cylinders (the coating's radius on the arris, as `Wear`'s), plastic past H, each point of line passing slides the contact's length 2a, not decision 17's R_eff·θ (about 100 times the contact); the rub in place as the lead turns about the axis is unchanged.
    - **What makes the edge tear, from the sources.** With this law an intact section does not tear at the edge at the published peel for any μ in the band up to about 160° (fiber-exit.md §2.3). What remains, by source:
      1. *A flaw.* The glass's strength over length (published: Prysmian AN221122): intrinsic about 4.8 GPa; every length proof-tested at 0.69 GPa, under which flaws break at 5 or fewer per 100 km; a second proof test breaks 10 % or less of that, at about the proof level; the extrinsic region is a Weibull line of far lower slope. Its slope, which sets how many surviving flaws per km lie under 3 GPa, was not found (IEC TR 62048's parameters are behind a paywall). `strength.flaw_spacing_m` (1 per 20 km, assumed, tied to "the rated payout is safe") stands. **The pack's peel front proof-tests every section again** before it reaches the edge (derived): E·r·√(2G/EI) is 0.10–2.16 GPa over the new band, on one side of the glass, so a flaw weaker than the peel's bend on that side breaks at the peel, not at the edge. An edge tear on a flaw needs the edge's stress on a section the peel left whole: a flaw on the other side, or one between the peel's and the edge's stress.
      2. *Wear within one flight.* The class's exit is an aluminium-alloy nozzle ([6], [7]); `tether.json` has it so (`spool.lip_archard_wear_coefficient`, the mild band). The groove is the counterface's wear, not the coating's: the coating (H 0.07 GPa, assumed) is 7–20 times softer than the alloy (0.5–1.5 GPa, anodised 3–5) and the glass never touches the metal, so the metal wears in the soft-on-hard regime (k ≤ 1e-8, assumed order, Hutchings 1992). Over 10 km under 0.1 N, and only while θ passes the bore's 9.7°, it loses at most 1e-14 m³, against 1.3e-12 m³ for a groove the coating fits at one azimuth (0.25 × 0.125 mm over a 40 µm contact; derived): **no groove forms in one flight**. A soft-plastic exit (H about 0.1 GPa, k 1e-5–1e-4) loses 1e-10 m³ in the same flight, a hundred grooves' worth: the published failure route [8], with the groove's wedge raising μ by π/2 to 1/sin(β/2) (fiber-exit.md §5.4). It is not the class's default, and is not modelled as one (the severe-k switch stays the known-wrong).
      3. So on the default exit the edge tears only on a flaw: the harness prints how often.
    - **The two liftoff causes: what loads the line** (`FiberCutTests.ProbeTheLipInTheCauses`, `FIBER_CASE=sharp|backward|backlow`, the session's drone).
      - *Sources.* No public source gives a drone canister's launch lay (the station's distance, slack left near the drone) or a payout tension at the start; the one spool maker's checklist that speaks to it says to accelerate slowly at takeoff because "otherwise, fiber is easily entangled into propellers", and to avoid rapid deceleration and rapid descent (fiber-exit.md [16], re-read tonight); another says to release the spool's strap and clips first. So the published route of a sharp liftoff's cut is **a rotor**, not the edge.
