# Design

## Context

- `define-map-format` provides:
  - the package format, the 9 surfaces and the catalog with primitive collision, materials, wind volumes and gaps
  - the D-009 terrain renderer
  - the loader, with micro-detail drawn from the physics generator
  - the golden file, at QueryVersion 2

  Everything visible is a placeholder.
- The reference notes (`terrain.md` §1–§11, `video-feed.md`) give sizes, colours measured from the footage, and a ranked build list. The pilot's answers add vehicles (ВАЗ, Lanos class, ЗИЛ, Урал), confirmed trenches, burnt and tilled fields, and the destroyed 5-storey block.
- The target look is judged at FPV distances and later through the analog feed, which carries about 450 × 286 lines of real detail. Silhouette, scale, colour, material response and clutter matter more than fine mesh detail.
- Tools: Blender 5.2.1 (headless Python), Godot 4.7.2 .NET.
- **Download permission (pilot, 2026-09-25):** about 20–25 CC0 texture sets plus 1–2 sky HDRIs, at 2K, roughly 200–400 MB, from polyhaven.com and ambientcg.com only. They're stored in Git LFS, pinned by checksum and listed in `CREDITS.md`. Nothing else may be downloaded without asking.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** convincing, physically described building blocks that the world map reuses unchanged; a gallery the pilot can judge quickly.
**Non-Goals:** photoreal close-ups in the clean view (the analog view is the product), unique hero assets, runtime destruction.

## Decisions

- **Procedural-plus-authored modelling in Blender, driven by scripts.**
  - Each asset is a Python builder script that constructs the mesh from measured dimensions: modules for walls, openings, roofs and damage cuts; body sections for vehicles.
  - The script saves the `.blend`, and the export script writes the `.glb`.
  - Parameters (size, damage level, state) come from the reference notes, so variants are cheap. That serves the pilot's "the map will change" and the need for abandoned and destroyed states.
  - Rejected: sculpting by hand, which agents can't do; downloading ready-made models, which usually need a login or have unclear licences.
- **Damage as a parameter:**
  - For houses and the block: roof stripped or partly collapsed, wall breaches, broken windows, soot, rubble spill.
  - For vehicles, the destroyed state is a separate variant: burnt-out shell with soot-black and rust paint, collapsed roof, missing panels and glass, sunk rims, scorch on the ground, debris spread. The abandoned state has flat tyres, dust, rust streaks, weeds through the wheel arches and faded paint.
  - Collision and gaps follow each variant, so a breached wall is a real fly-through gap.
- **Textures:**
  - CC0 PBR sets (albedo, normal, roughness, AO) from Poly Haven and ambientCG, fetched by `tools/assets/fetch_cc0.py`. Each item is pinned by id, resolution and SHA-256, and listed in `CREDITS.md`.
  - At most 2K, authored at the photo albedos from the notes, then checked against the notes' "feed" colours through the analog preview once it exists.
  - Surfaces use texture arrays, as the loader already does. Tilled uses a furrow albedo and normal oriented along each cell's ridge azimuth, filtered and faded by distance to stop aliasing.
- **Burnt field surface:**
  - new surface `burnt_field`: firm dark loam under a thin brittle char layer, sparse charred stubble, high dust
  - its values are proposed by the world artist and signed off by the physics engineer, the same way as the other 9
  - a minor data addition, not a format change
- **Terrain holes:**
  - an optional `holes.png` (1-bit per 0.5 m cell), with a format minor bump to 1.1
  - the renderer discards holed fragments; terrain collision uses a HeightMapShape3D with holes (NaN heights)
  - `SampleGround` sets `Hole` and hands the ground over to the placed trench or cellar mesh
  - trench meshes' collision boxes carry a `surface:<id>` material, so their walls are soil (review X-7)
  - QueryVersion goes to 3, and the golden file is re-recorded
- **Micro-detail view:**
  - fade the near ring with a dithered alpha by distance over its last 2 m
  - add a far layer: density-only instanced cards or short blades from the cover map, a cheap shader, out to 60–100 m, fading in under the near ring
  - the near ring stays generated by the physics generator (parity unchanged)
- **Gallery layout:**
  - a 512 m map, assembled by a generator script so the pilot's feedback turns into edits
  - terrain patches around the edge, asset stations along two roads, each with open space for orbiting and flying through
  - labels via a small UI node reading the station list
- **Order of work,** so the pilot sees progress early:
  1. pipeline, textures and surfaces
  2. the adobe house and tree belt, the most-used pieces
  3. vehicles
  4. the block
  5. everything else
  6. the gallery

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Scripted modelling looks too clean or regular] → Damage and weathering parameters, randomised per instance from a seed; decals for soot, rust and stains; debris scatter. Judged at UAT-1 by the pilot.
- [CC0 libraries lack a specific material, e.g. Soviet silicate brick or asbestos sheet] → Derive it from the closest CC0 set by tinting to the footage colours, and note the derivation in `CREDITS.md`.
- [LFS size and quota] → 2K cap, shared texture sets, a size report per asset in the export. Keep UAT-1 under about 500 MB. Unused sources aren't committed.
- [Holes touch the world query and golden file] → Implemented and checked first, with the physics engineer's sign-off, before any asset depends on them.
- [Frame budget with the block plus the tree belt] → LODs, impostors for far trees, and one measured busiest view in the gallery spec.
