# Design: p1-l01-l03-pole-wires

## Context
The pipeline after `p1-pipeline-hardening` and `p1-s04-belt-straw`: ranges, the two-sided audit (with micro meshes skipped), `derive.py`, cylinder colliders, `surface:<id>`. The sheet gives every pole and wire number (§L01, §L03, the shared tables): log 9.5 m (7.5 m above ground, 2.0 m buried), top 0.16 m, butt 0.26 m, hooks of 16–18 mm bar 0.5 m apart, insulators 0.108 × 0.075 m, brace 0.16 m at 25° attached at 0.65, stub 4.3 m with 1.6 m showing, lean 9° (L); wires 5 / 9 / 20 mm, 40 m span, sag 2 %, 0.5 m spacing, top at 7.0 m; the sag–tension table (T = w·L²/8f, k = 4T/L). Labels below are for the numbers this design adds.

## Goals / Non-Goals
**Goals:** two small generators that exercise the whole metadata contract; a wire that is geometry and a record the map will read; the thin-wire look the pilot judges at G1.
**Non-Goals:** see proposal; no line placement, no L02, no strike physics.

## Decisions
1. **One `pole` generator for L01 and L02.** `pole_kind` selects the round log (this change) or the rectangular concrete stand (L02's later task adds its branch); heads, hooks, brace, stub, lean and the `damage` dial are shared, exactly as the sheet's shared table. Only `wood_log` and `wood_on_stub` are built and tested here; `concrete_sv` raises "not built yet" in `check` so a params file cannot ask for it.
2. **Pole geometry.** The log is a tapered tube of 12 sides at LOD0 (derived: a 0.26 m butt at 12 sides deviates 1.6 mm from round, under the 10 mm the sheet asks for), 8 at LOD1, a 6-sided prism at LOD2; weathering checks as a derived grain map along the pole (the sheet's derived map: a grain-along-the-pole albedo and a damage mask R gouges, G char, B weathering) from the chosen log set; hooks are bent 17 mm bars (6-sided tubes), insulators 8-sided lathe shapes, bands 6-turn coils simplified to a torus each. The brace foots on a buried sill (a box flush with the ground). The origin is the ground line at the foot; burial is below y = 0 and is not drawn (nothing is under the gallery floor).
   **As built (task 1.1):** a 12-gon whose corners stand 2/(1 + cos 15°) of the radius out, so its faces average the true radius (within 2.3 mm of round at the 0.26 m butt; the 1.6 mm above was a slip, an inscribed 12-gon is 4.4 mm inside). Nothing is drawn below y = 0: every member reaching the ground (log, brace, stub, grounding wire) is cut by that plane and left open there, so the review stage and the map need no lift. No sill is drawn: a sill flush with the ground lies wholly under it. The generator is `PLANTED`, and the audit gained one opt-in rule (`audit.run(..., planted=True)`, from the generator's `PLANTED`): the half-space under y = 0 covers primitives as another primitive would, clipped before the search, so the log's and the brace's buried capsule caps and the stub's buried box are no invisible walls (a cap below an open tube otherwise stands r √2 off it, 0.17 m on this log). No derived grain or damage map: `rough_wood`'s checks already run along V, which runs along the grain; the damage mask comes with D and X. The lean is a rigid rotation about the foot; with a brace only across it (azimuth 90 or 270), about the line through both feet, so both stay in the ground and the brace triangle keeps its shape. The line runs along X: hooks zig-zag on ±Z (hook_0 the top one on +Z), the crossarm spans Z on the -X face, the brace and the stub stand on +X, the grounding wire runs down -X. Bands are flat straps (30 × 6 mm) round the convex outline of log and stub.
3. **Collision.** Log: 3 capsules (0–2.5, 2.5–5, 5–7.5 m above ground) with radii at the mid-height of each (derived: a linear taper from 0.13 to 0.08 m deviates at most 8 mm from a mid-radius capsule over 2.5 m, inside the sheet's 10 mm), lean and all; brace: one capsule; stub: one box; crossarm: a box; each hook: a capsule r 0.0085 m; each insulator: a cylinder r 0.0375 × 0.108 m. Materials as the sheet. Gap: the brace triangle (pole, brace, ground) as a planar polygon with `narrowest_m` from the inscribed circle; the sheet's 1.78 m is the check. Wind: one box around the pole, porosity 0. Snag: `hook` per hook (r 8 mm), `edge` per insulator neck (r 5 mm), crossarm ends (2 mm), bands (2 mm), the grounding wire (3 mm). Ports: one per insulator, id `hook_<n>`, direction outward from the pole axis.
   **As built (task 1.1):**
   - *Log:* equal segments at their mid radius, three, or more when the taper needs them to stay within 10 mm (the 7.5 m log on a stub tapers 0.13 → 0.08 m over 7.5 m: four); the lower ones capsules, the top one a **cylinder**, because the top is a flat saw cut that a capsule's cap stands 25–35 mm off. Measured by the audit on the log alone: 8.6 mm (straight), 8.5 mm (stub), both ways. The above-ground radius tapers 0.1195 → 0.08 m on the 9.5 m log (the butt is 2 m under the ground), not 0.13 → 0.08.
   - *Hooks:* two capsules each (the horizontal leg and the pin), since one capsule cannot follow an L within 50 mm; within 7.2 mm. Crossarm pins one capsule each. Insulators cylinders 0.0375 × 0.108 m, 19.9 mm off the neck groove. Porcelain is `glass` (the sheet, after the physics sign-off), not `masonry`.
   - *Gap:* the brace triangle is the **real clear space** (the pipeline's gap convention: the pole's inner surface, the brace's inner surface and the ground, in the brace's plane), so `narrowest_m` is **1.611 m**, not the sheet's 1.78 m. The 1.78 m is the triangle of the two *axes* with the attachment rounded to 4.9 m (1.778 m; at 0.65 × 7.5 = 4.875 m it is 1.769 m, already 11 mm off); the sheet's own clear-width formula 2.28 · (1 − y / 4.9) − 0.18 gives about 1.63 m. The scenario "Brace gap against the drone" should read 1.611 m; the test checks the clear triangle by hand, the audit's circle (1.6107 m) and traces the 1.78 m to the axes. The id records the heights: `brace_triangle_any_yaw_below_2.95m_nose_first_below_3.24m` (the sheet: 3.0 and 3.3 m).
   - *Snag records* carry the `material` of the member they follow (hook and pin `steel`, neck `glass`, crossarm end `timber`, band `steel`, grounding wire `cable`), ahead of p1-v01-locust's rule.
   - *Ports* sit at the bottom of the neck groove on the side away from the pole, 0.019 m off the insulator's axis; a conductor's centre lies one radius further along `direction`. Straight and stub: `hook_0`–`hook_3` at 7.355, 6.855, 6.355 and 5.855 m (the top hook 0.3 m under the 7.5 m top, the sheet's 0.2–0.4 m; the insulator 0.08 m up the hook, its neck 0.075 m up it), not the L03 scenario's 7.0 m.
   **As built (task 1.7, the readings' rework):**
   - *Ports* carry `max_diameter_m` 0.014 m, the TF-20 groove's width (0.068–0.082 m up the insulator; task 1.5's reading §1). A bare pin (`insulator` none) seats no conductor and writes no port. `L03_thick`'s 15 mm drop is over it: it hangs from `hook_2` for G1 (a wire has no collider) and waits for a clamp port (the `sip_bracket` head, not built).
   - *Snag records* (reading §6): hooks and pins 8.5 mm, the bar's half thickness and their capsules' radius; the insulator's ring moves from the neck (13.5 mm inside the 0.0375 m cylinder, out of the fiber's reach) to the skirt's drip edge, 0.0325 m from the axis and 0.005 m up its base with 5 mm, its reach flush with the cylinder's side and bottom at the ring's 8 corners (2.5 mm inside between them); bands on the strap's centre line, 3.015 mm out of the outline (half the 6 mm strap, square to the edges), with 3 mm, flush with both faces.
   - *The crossarm* hangs `crossarm_below_m` under the top (a param, 0.1–2.0 m, default 1.5 m: L 1–3 measure 1.2–2 m), and with `crossarm_0p4` the hooks stand above it, from the top down (0.3 m under the top, every 0.5 m); `check` refuses hooks whose lowest bar would not clear the crossarm's insulators. Ports run from the top: the hooks', then the crossarm's two pins. `L01_leaning`: the crossarm's top 6.0 m up the log, its pins' ports at 6.125 m, the hooks' at 7.355 and 6.855 m (upright frame), leaned 9°.
   - *The brace* leaves `L01_leaning` (L 1–3 show none) for a new end pole, `L01_braced` (the straight pole with `brace` single). Its gap is the same clear triangle, `narrowest_m` 1.610885 m (the audit's circle 1.6107 m). The id gives the heights above the ground (reading §4): each in-plane height placed with the lean, so the upright end pole reads `brace_triangle_any_yaw_below_2.95m_nose_first_below_3.24m` and the same pole leaned 9° across its brace `..._2.91m_..._3.20m`.
4. **LODs** (derived from 935 px/rad): LOD1 at 35 m (an 0.075 m insulator under 2 px), LOD2 at 120 m (a 17 mm hook bar is gone long before; the pole's 0.16 m top under 1.5 px), cull 1 200 m (the sheet: at least 1 km for the night silhouette; the pole at 1 km is 0.16 m / 0.17 px wide but 7.5 m / 7 px tall, so it still reads).
   **As built (task 1.3):** the distances hold (`L01_leaning`'s LOD heights 0.1836 / 0.0536 / 0.0054), but the opaque LOD2 prism does not read as the line above assumes. Once it is under a pixel across (from about 190 m at 935 px/rad; 54 m at the feed's 270), the project's MSAA 1 draws it only where it covers a pixel centre: at 200 m against the night sky it is a dashed line (5 empty rows of its 30) and at 1 km it draws nothing or one pixel, by a quarter-pixel shift. The night landmark aliases and pops instead of fading; task 1.4's coverage applied to the pole's far LODs (or MSAA) would fix it, a tech-artist change outside this task.
   **As built (task 1.7):** LOD1 and LOD2 are drawn with task 1.4's thin-wire coverage. Every member there is a round bar whose corners lie on its circle at the local radius, each with its radial unit normal and that radius as its second UV, all of it triangles (as the wire tubes, so the custom normals survive the FBX); the crossarm and the stub, boxes at LOD0, are 4-sided round bars there of half their narrower side (0.05 and 0.085 m: the width a level look sees across the crossarm, and the stub's across the line), so the far pole is never drawn wider than it is. LOD0 is unchanged (byte-identical meshes for the straight and stub poles; the leaning pole loses only its brace). The importer draws every slot whose vertices all carry a radius in uv2 with `EnsureThinWireMaterial` of its recipe (`pole_wood_thin_wire.mat`, `pole_concrete_thin_wire.mat`), every other slot with the recipe's URP/Lit; the far LODs ask for shadows and the shader, which has no shadow caster, gives none, so the pole's shadow ends at 35 m. Checked in an uncommitted scratch view: `L01_leaning`'s LOD2 against L's night sky (#5d5e5d) at 935 px/rad, MSAA 1, a half-float colour buffer, across and along the line, each over four quarter-pixel camera shifts. No row is empty at 200 m (33 rows) or 1 km (5 rows). The rows' coverage sums are 0.934–0.943 px at 200 m and 0.184 px at 1 km, against the log's 935 · 2r / d of 0.929–0.942 and 0.182–0.184 px (within 0.2–1.6 %), so they fall by five from 200 m to 1 km as the distance grows by five, and they hold within 0.2 % over the shifts (each row within 0.3 %). With the coverage off, the same view breaks as task 1.3 saw: 5–7 empty rows of 33 at 200 m along the line, 23 empty rows or a full pixel across it by the shift, and nothing in three shifts of four at 1 km. Triangles per LOD: `L01_straight` 892 / 22 / 16, `L01_leaning` 808 / 34 / 16, `L01_braced` 916 / 38 / 16, `L01_stub` 1 206 / 32 / 16.
5. **`wire_span` builds in the span's own frame.** Origin at the first attachment's foot, +X along the span, `attach_heights_m` for the two ends, `span_m` the horizontal distance. The catenary is sampled at `points_per_m` (default 2: 81 points for 40 m; derived: the chord error of a 2 % sag catenary between points 0.5 m apart is under 0.1 mm) and the tube is a 6-sided sweep at LOD0 (derived: a 9 mm wire at 6 sides deviates 0.6 mm), 4-sided at LOD1 (from 20 m), culled where the thin-wire material's coverage falls under 1/255 (the material decides the fade; the mesh is kept to 300 m, assumed). SIP bundles are two or four tubes twisted at `twist_pitch_m`. Damage: a `cut` conductor splits into two catenary tails hanging from their ports (a chain line under its own weight, the free end at `cut_hang_m` below the port, assumed 0.5–3 m), a `down` conductor lies on y = 0 as a straight line with the sag's slack in gentle bends; G1 builds intact only.
   **As built (task 1.2):**
   - *Frame and ends:* the origin is the ground under the first pole's axis, +X to the second pole's axis at `span_m`; both poles stand as L01 does. A conductor's end is its port plus one radius along the port's direction (resting in the neck groove). With `vertical_zigzag`, conductor n hangs at `attach_heights_m` (now `{start, end}`, the params having no list type) less n · `spacing_m`, on +Z for n even, its port `offset_m` + n · `offset_step_m` from the pole's axis: two params beyond the sheet's table, so the ends land on `L01_straight`'s ports (hook_0 0.250579 m out, each next one 2.632 mm further as the log thickens; within 1.3 µm). The sheet's `class` is checked against the diameter (thin 3–6.5, medium 6.5–12.5, thick 12.5–32 mm, the sheet's bands split midway so A-25's 6.4 mm stays thin).
   - *Catenary:* y(u) = y_a + 2c sinh((u − 2m)/2c) sinh(u/2c) (the difference form of c cosh, free of cancellation), c by bisection on log c so the curve hangs `sag_ratio` · `span_m` below the chord at mid-span, sampled evenly in horizontal distance. The chord error at 0.5 m is **0.13 mm**, not under 0.1 mm (c = 250.13 m for 0.8 m on 40 m: 0.5² / 8c).
   - *Tubes (task 1.4's contract):* every ring corner lies on the conductor's circle with its radial unit normal (smooth shading through custom normals) and the radius as its second UV, (r, 0) m, which Unity reads as uv2; `lib/geometry.Mesh` gained per-vertex normals and second UVs and `lib/export` writes them (a second UV map, custom normals on the faces whose every vertex has one), and nothing else changes for a mesh without them (K01, L01, S04, V13 export byte-identical). Both ends capped, the cap corners sharing the radial normals so the shader's widening never splits the tube. The tubes are written as triangles: Blender keeps a custom normal relative to its corner fan, and the FBX export's own triangulation re-cut the end rings' fans and turned their normals by up to 45° (checked by an FBX round trip: every corner now within 0.1° of its radial normal, the second UV map intact). The audit measures 0.000 mm on the bare wires. Two LODs: 6 sides, 4 sides from 20 m. The cull follows the diameter (task 1.4): where the thin-wire line's column sum, 935 · D / d px, falls under 1 % of a pixel, so 467.5 m for 5 mm, 841.5 m for 9 mm, 1 870 m for 20 mm (281 m at 3 mm, always past LOD1); `lod.cull_m` is gone from the params. Triangles: `L03_thin` 968 / 644, `L03_medium` 3 872 / 2 576, `L03_thick` 8 656 / 324.
   - *SIP:* `sip_drop` 2 cores and `sip_main` 4, each D sin(π/k)/(1 + sin(π/k)) across (10 mm in a 20 mm drop, its centre 5 mm off the axis), twisted at 8 rings a turn at LOD0 (`L03_thick`: 360 rings per core, each core its own tube with its own 5 mm radius in uv2, 0.238 mm off the bundle's envelope), one 4-sided tube of the bundle's diameter at LOD1. Every other type is one strand.
   - *Damage:* only intact is built; `damage` is one state for the span (cut and down refused by check), not a list per conductor, until L04 and L05 build those states. `seed` offsets the UVs along each conductor and turns a bundle's twist.
   - *Slots and materials:* `steel` (wire_steel: steel_wire), `aluminium` (wire_aluminium: bare_al, acsr), `insulated` (wire_sip: the SIP types and insulated), each physics `cable`. `wire_aluminium` and `wire_steel` are now flat `palette` recipes (#8e9193 and #5e4a3a, smoothness 0.41 and 0.48, each its former set's mean 1 − roughness), as `wire_sip` was, so the thin-wire material never samples a 24×-gained tread plate; L01's grounding wire (wire_steel) turns flat with them, at the same mean colour. `metal_plate` is pinned but unused.
6. **The `wires` record.** Per conductor: `points` (the sampled centre line), `diameter_m`, `mass_kg_per_m` (the conductor preset), `sag_m`, `material: "cable"`, `damage`. The map compiler (phase 3) copies them into `objects.json` with the asset's placement; PHY-22 derives T = w·L²/8f and k = 4T/L from them, so nothing in the physics re-reads the mesh. Alternative rejected: capsules per segment in `collision` (80 shapes per wire, and the physics review already settled wires as map objects, D-007).
   **As built (task 1.2):** the record is `slot`, `diameter_m`, `mass_kg_per_m`, `sag_m`, `material`, `damage`, `points` (in that order, the long list last), right after `ports` in the metadata. `slot` is added: the material slot its tube is drawn in, the link the audit and the importer use (a slot of physics `cable`). `sag_m` is the design sag below the chord at mid-span (ratio × span). A SIP bundle is one record at the bundle's diameter and mass. The validator refuses a record without every field, fewer than two points, a material other than `cable`, a diameter or mass not positive or a negative sag, a damage state outside intact, cut and down, and a slot that is not a `cable` slot of `materials`. Each conductor also writes an `edge` snag along the same points, radius D/2, material `cable`.
7. **Audit and import.** The audit matches each tube's vertices to the nearest polyline segment (distance minus radius ≤ 5 mm) and removes them from the primitive pass; `CatalogImporter` assigns the thin-wire material to wire renderers and skips colliders for them; `AssetPhysicsMeta` gains `ports` and `wires` lists.
   **As built (task 1.2):** the faces of the wires' slots are a wire's tube: they leave the primitive pass, and every vertex of them goes to the wire of its slot it lies nearest outside of; its deviation is its distance to the point list less the radius (zero inside), the largest per wire printed (`audit wires[0] 9 mm (slot aluminium): largest deviation 0.000 mm at ... over 486 vertices, tolerance 5 mm`); a wire no vertex goes to has no tube and fails. The importer reads `wires` into `AssetPhysicsMeta.wires` and logs them; a wire's slot draws with `EnsureThinWireMaterial` of its recipe's material (task 1.4's `<recipe>_thin_wire.mat`, merged from its sub-branch to build against), every vertex of that submesh must carry uv2.x > 0 or the import fails, a renderer whose every slot is a wire casts no shadow, and no collider stands for a wire (they never enter `collision`). The recipe's own URP/Lit material stays for L01's grounding wire. The importer also clears a texture reference whose asset is gone (`wire_steel`'s dropped mask stayed in its material as a missing map).
   **As built (task 1.7, the physics reading's §8):**
   - *The record* gains `axial_stiffness_n` (EA) and `breaking_force_n` (the rated minimum) after `sag_m`, both required and positive, from a per-type table in `wire_span.py`: E 200 GPa for steel and 63 GPa for aluminium (assumed); the metallic section, solid steel π D²/4, bare aluminium A-50's published 49.5 mm² in 9.0 mm scaled by D² (its 0.778 fill), the SIP drop its two 16 mm² cores; the strength, ГОСТ 3282's 390 N/mm² floor for steel and A-50's published 8 198 N over 49.5 mm² (165.6 N/mm²) for aluminium, assumed for the SIP cores. `L03_thin` 3.927 MN and 7 658 N, `L03_medium` 3.119 MN and 8 198 N, `L03_thick` 2.016 MN and 5 300 N. `acsr`, `sip_main` and `insulated` have no row and are refused by `check` until their data is read.
   - *Wire snags* carry `edge_radius_m` where the fiber bends over something sharper than the conductor (reading §6): a 7-strand bare conductor's outer strand, D/6 (A-50: 1.5 mm), a SIP bundle's core (3.75 mm in the 15 mm drop), none on solid steel. The validator takes it as optional, positive and at most `radius_m`.
   - *The audit's tolerance* is min(5 mm, D/2), printed per wire (reading §3): 2.5 mm on the 5 mm wire, 4.5 mm on the 9 mm, 5 mm on the 15 mm drop; the built tubes measure 0.000, 0.000 and 0.243 mm.
8. **Thin-wire rendering** (tech-artist, `Shaders/ThinWire.shader`): an unlit-plus-lit URP shader for the tube that, in the vertex stage, expands the tube to at least one pixel's width when its projected diameter is below one pixel and multiplies the alpha by the covered fraction (projected diameter / pixel size, the sheet's 270·D/d at the feed's 935 px/rad), so a wire fades with distance instead of breaking into sparkles; no minimum visible width, no line primitive. Alpha blending, no shadows, the base colour from the recipe's tint. Checked by the 10 / 50 / 200 m tiles of `L03_thin` and at 1080p in the gallery; the feed's own 450 × 286 downsample is phase 4's.
9. **Materials.** The log: `rough_wood` or `weathered_brown_planks` at 1k (derived: a 0.8 m circumference log at 512 px/m is 410 px around), chosen at the comparison against the sheet's grey-silver #8a8478 with the derived grain and damage mask; stub `rough_concrete` 1k; hooks, bands, steel wire `rusty_metal` 1k; bare aluminium `metal_plate` 1k tinted #8e9193 with a derived stranding normal along the wire; SIP a plain #1f2022 rough material; porcelain a plain glazed #d8d6cf, glass #6f9c95 (both smooth, no set).
   **As built (task 1.1):** all five sets pinned at 1k; tile sizes from Poly Haven's published dimensions (rough_wood 0.5 m, weathered_brown_planks 1.8 m, rough_concrete 1.23 m, rusty_metal 1.5 m, metal_plate 0.5 m). `pole_wood` uses `rough_wood` for now (grey checked wood, its grain along V), tiled [0.5, 2.0] m: stretched 4× along the grain so the checks run long as on a pole and repeat under four times up its 7.5 m; the wrap round the log closes on a whole tile, so no seam. `weathered_brown_planks` is pinned for the comparison but shows plank joints across the grain. Tints land each set's measured linear mean on the sheet's albedo, written as sRGB: wood #6d6459 → #8a8478 [1.249, 1.292, 1.312]; concrete #bab79e → #9a978e [0.833, 0.831, 0.903]; `pole_steel` and `wire_steel` rusty_metal #a46432 → the sheet's rusted steel #5e4a3a [0.584, 0.757, 1.138] (no hook colour is given; assumed the same); `wire_aluminium` metal_plate #3f331d → #8e9193 [2.094, 2.509, 3.897], a gain of up to 24× in linear blue over a diamond tread plate (0.23 % clipped), so 1.2/1.4 should prefer a flat recipe for bare aluminium if the thin-wire material samples the base map. SIP and porcelain are `palette` recipes (flat #1f2022 smoothness 0.35, #d8d6cf smoothness 0.85). Metallic 0 throughout (weathered, oxidised surfaces). No glass recipe: `insulator` glass is refused by `check`. No stranding normal is derived.
   **As built (task 1.3):** the log keeps `rough_wood`. `weathered_brown_planks` is boards whose joints run 0.13 m apart across the grain, which on a log would read as staves; `rough_wood` is grey checked timber with its checks along the grain, and the log renders #7e786e on average under the review's overcast light (the tinted #8a8478 less the set's ambient occlusion): the sheet's grey-silver. `weathered_brown_planks` and `metal_plate` stay pinned but unused (unpinning them is a separate step). No params or tints changed. L is a night feed and shows no material, so `weathering` stays unbuilt at 0.5: every G1 pole is the I state, weathered. A wire against a bright sky is under a pixel across, so its shade is its coverage times its contrast, and its albedo barely moves it. Seen 22 m off against J's sky (#d6dfe7), the 20 mm SIP drop is darkest #a3a9b0. J's #afb5be is 0.8 of that darkening: a 16 mm drop at the same distance (the sheet's 2 × 16 drop is 15 mm), or ours 28 m off. The 5 mm steel wire 11 m off is darkest #b9bec2, and the drop against D's backlit haze #2a2b2d on #adaaa4 at 3.5 m (D 12: #1e1816). All of these use the sheet's albedos.
   **As built (task 1.7):** the unused `weathered_brown_planks` and `metal_plate` pins are dropped (manifest, their files, `CREDITS.md` regenerated): five sets became three for this change (`rough_wood`, `rough_concrete`, `rusty_metal`).
10. **Params files.** `assets-src/lines/L01_straight.json`, `L01_leaning.json` (`lean_deg` 9, `brace` single), `L01_stub.json` (`pole_kind` wood_on_stub), from the shared table with ranges and effects in `PARAMS`; `L03_thin.json` (5 mm, `steel_wire`, 1 conductor), `L03_medium.json` (9 mm, `bare_al`, 4 conductors, `vertical_zigzag`), `L03_thick.json` (20 mm, `sip_drop`, 1 bundle, 20 m span). `check` refuses a sag that brings a conductor under the clearance the sheet gives for its type unless `damage` is set.
