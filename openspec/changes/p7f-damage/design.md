# Design: p7f-damage

## Context
- **The rotor as built** (`p6-flight-core` 1.5–1.7, `Propulsion`): a DC motor per corner at 4 kHz, i = (d·V − Ke·ω)/R, J·ω̇ = Kt·i − Kt·I0 − Q, Kt = Ke = 1/Kv (900 KV, published; R 0.11 Ω derived; I0 1.0 A assumed, so Kt·I0 = 0.0106 N·m; J 6.0e-5 kg·m² derived, ±30 %); the ESC's 40 A current limit (assumed policy, D-051, D-053); APC 10x4.5MR's published C_T and C_P against rpm and advance ratio J; the stall torque at the limit is Kt·40 A = 0.42 N·m (derived). The rotor's energy at 9000 rpm is ½Jω² = 26.6 J (derived).
- **The prop:** 2 blades (measured, T), R 0.127 m (measured), 16 g (published), the sheet's chord 26 mm at 0.3 R tapering to 8 mm at the tip (18 mm at 0.1 R assumed), about 11 % thick at 0.3 R (assumed, p7b 1.3c), black glass-filled nylon (`drone.md`); the flap model's K_β = I_b·(ω₀² + (K_s − 1)·Ω²) with ω₀ 150 Hz (assumed) and the blade sections' a 5.7/rad, α_L0 −6.3° and c_d0 (p7c 1.6's fit to APC's static thrust and κ).
- **The asymmetry** (`p7b-airframe` 1.1): `prop.unbalance`, U uniform in [0, U_per], U_per = m·G/Ω = 0.107 g·mm at G 6.3 (published, Tyto Robotics; ISO 21940-11), pulling each hub with U·ω² at `RotorAngle`: 0.095 N at 9000 rpm (derived).
- **The disc** (`ContactModel`, 1.6 and 1.6b): a bounding sphere, four chord capsules, a 12-capsule ring round the rim; a pair is a `PropStrike` event and carries no force; an entry faster than 3 m/s on a rigid row is a `Crash`. On the map (1.6b) the drone at 10 m/s ends its flight by a prop crash on `L01_straight` (timber), `H01_D`'s wall (masonry), V01's ash (timber) and L03's conductor (cable); `R01_A`'s sheet metal is compliant, so its prop strike is not a crash. `IWorldQuery.Geometry(obj, shape)` already returns any shape's exact geometry (plane, box, sphere, capsule, cylinder, wire).
- **The motors' build** (`drone.md`, published): Ø37.2 × 32 mm, a bell about 24 mm tall over a 6 mm base (measured T), stator 31 × 15 mm 12N14P, a 5 mm shaft, NSK/NMB 5 × 11 × 5 bearings (685ZZ: C0r 276 N, Cr 714 N, NMB's published data).
- **The seam** (`p7a-ground` design 9): each element strike is written in its step to `FlightState.BladeStrikes` (per rotor and blade) and logged as `PropStrike`; 7a applies the strike's mechanics and never damages a blade; 7f reads the slot in the same step and adds strikes on shapes to it.
- **The rule:** randomness only as a bounded perturbation of a named physical parameter, from a named, logged stream; never noise on the pose; the ground and the world are never drawn.

## Goals / Non-Goals
**Goals:** PHY-26 physical, logged and replayable: blades and motors damaged by each strike's energy, geometry and materials, flying on worse; the pilot's account ("worse after a scratch") and the published checks met, each target failing a known-wrong model; determinism, 0 B and PLT-04 kept.
**Non-Goals:** the proposal's.

## Decisions

1. **The blade as a structure** (`drone.json`'s new `props.blade`, physics-owned beside asset-artist's parts, reviewed by the asset artist; DroneSpec's loader, the header).
   - **Stations:** 40 equal spans from 0.1 R to the tip (assumed; 1.2 checks the tables against 160). Per station the chord c(x) (the sheet's, above), the thickness t(x) (11 % of the chord at 0.3 R to 8 % at the tip, assumed, scaled so the two blades and the hub make the published 16 g; the hub about 2 g, derived from its Ø19 × 8 mm), the section area 0.7·c·t (an airfoil's fill, assumed), the section angle (the 4.5-inch helix less α_L0).
   - **The material** (glass-filled nylon, PA66-GF30 class; published orders from BASF Ultramid A3WG6's data, dry: modulus 9–10 GPa, stress at break 170–190 MPa, Charpy notched 9–13 kJ/m², unnotched 60–95 kJ/m², density about 1.36 g/cm³): `density`, `flexural_strength_pa` σ_f (fracture of a section in bending), `crush_strength_pa` σ_c (the blade crushed against a harder face, assumed of σ_f's order), `fracture_energy_j_per_m2` G_c (from the notched Charpy energy per ligament area), `proportional_limit` (the share of σ_f past which a section takes a permanent set, assumed 0.7: the class is quasi-brittle, with 3–5 % strain at break), `hardness_pa` H_b (ball-indentation order, about 0.2 GPa, assumed). In-service props carry moisture; task 1.1 states whether the conditioned values (published direction: lower modulus and strength) or the dry ones are used, and why.
   - **Each blade's state** (eight, preallocated, a function of the flight like the contact latches): the tip radius x_tip (1 new); per station the chord share left (1 new: a leading-edge chip removes from the front), the pitch set Δθ and flap set z (0 new); the mass lost and its first moments. Every change is a `BladeDamaged` event (rotor, blade, mode, station range, mass lost, the cause's strike).
   - **Sections' strength:** flatwise M_f(x) = σ_f·c·t²/6 and edgewise M_e(x) = σ_f·t·c²/6, times the fill's form factor (assumed 0.7), on what is left of the chord (derived, Euler–Bernoulli).

2. **A damaged rotor's aerodynamics** (PHY-26: "a chipped, bent or shortened blade loses thrust"; `DamagedRotor`).
   - **The model:** blade-element momentum theory on both blades' remaining span, linear lift (a, α_L0, c_d0 as p7c 1.6), climb inflow λ_c = J/π, the annulus's inflow from momentum on the blades present there, λ = √((σa/16 − λ_c/2)² + σaθx/8) − (σa/16 − λ_c/2) (Leishman 2006, the BEMT closed form, published; √ only), σ the annulus's solidity from the chords left; each blade's lift integrated to its own effective tip B·x_tip with Sissingh's B = 1 − √(2C_T)/N_b (published, Leishman 2006), its torque from the induced and profile terms (published BET). Each blade's pitch set and its station chords enter its own sections.
   - **The tables:** when a rotor's blades change (a damage event, never in steady state), BEMT runs at the 16 advance ratios of APC's table (at the reference 9000 rpm; the ratio's rpm dependence left out, assumed): thrust and torque ratios η_T(J), η_Q(J) against the intact sheet blade, each blade's thrust share and centroid, each blade's torque share and centroid. Propulsion's T and Q become the table's times η, linear between the points, so a sound rotor without the factory pitch is exactly APC's (the ratio path is skipped and the goldens keep their bits).
   - **What it gives** (a simplified BEMT at 9000 rpm, derived here and re-derived in 1.2): 5 mm off one tip costs that blade 6.6 % and the rotor 3.3 % of its thrust at a fixed speed; 12.7 mm (5 % R) off one tip 16.6 % and 7.8 %; 5 mm off both 6.2 %. The outer span carries the lift (∝ x²), so a trim costs far more than its share of the span.
   - **The once-per-revolution loads at the hub,** each turning with `RotorAngle` (spin-signed), summed at each 4 kHz sub-step beside today's unbalance, into the rigid body's force and moment (the step's mean):
     - **mass:** U·ω² outward at U's angle, U the vector sum of the factory unbalance and each blade's lost mass's first moment (−Δm·r̄ at that blade's azimuth) (Newton, derived). 5 mm off one tip is about 0.03–0.06 g at 0.12 m, so 3–6 N at hover against the factory's 0.095 N; 12.7 mm about 10–20 N (derived; the spread is the tip's assumed thickness, pinned by 1.1's mass fit).
     - **couple:** each blade's flap set and a bent shaft's tilt put mass out of the disc's plane: ω²·Σ m_k r_k z_k about the blade's perpendicular (derived: the centrifugal moment of the displaced stations).
     - **aerodynamic:** each blade's thrust T_b at its centroid r̄_b: the hub moment Σ_b T_b r̄_b about the axis perpendicular to blade b and the shaft, and each blade's drag Q_b/r̄_Qb along its motion: an in-plane force; both cancel on a sound prop (derived). 5 mm off one tip makes 0.04 N·m, 12.7 mm 0.10 N·m at hover (derived, above).
   - **The rotor's inertia and mass:** J less Σ Δm·r² of what is lost (derived: a 5 mm tip is about 0.1 % of J, a lost blade about 40 %), so the motor's lag and spin momentum follow; the body's mass build is unchanged (assumed: a lost blade's 6–7 g is 0.14 % of 4.8 kg and moves the centre of mass under 0.3 mm, derived).
   - **Where it goes:** the rigid body, so the gyro, the controller (its RPM filter's first harmonic notches 1/rev, as the builder's chain does), the frame's modes by their inertial relief (`AirframeModes`), the camera's pose and `VibrationBands`; `p7e-camera-feel` reads them there.

3. **A new prop's factory imbalance** (PHY-07, D-059: the baseline shake).
   - **Mass:** `prop.unbalance` as built (G 6.3, U uniform in [0, U_per], uniform angle), now the blade state's initial unbalance.
   - **Aerodynamic** (new stream `prop.blade_pitch`): each blade's sections + Δθ, Δθ uniform in ±0.25° (assumed: no blade-to-blade pitch tolerance is published for moulded props; the bound gives a 1/rev hub moment of about 0.02 N·m at its edge (±0.3° gives 0.0225 N·m, derived), the order of the mass unbalance's moment about the centre of mass at the arm, 0.095 N × 0.226 m = 0.021 N·m (derived), so neither share is imperceptible). Eight draws at the flight's start, in the header with their bound. The tables run once per rotor at arming (allocation-free: the buffers exist).
   - **Two effects:** the blades' difference is a 1/rev moment (the baseline shake); their mean is a per-rotor thrust offset (±0.3° gives +1.5 %, derived), a steady moment that joins PHY-07's Kv yank. PHY-07's band (`AsymmetryScenario.Predict`) is recomputed with it; "with asymmetry off the drift is at least halved" must still hold.

4. **Where a blade meets a shape** (`BladeStrike`, the contact geometry).
   - **The pairs:** the disc's gate and ring find a pair as now. On its first touch physics reads the shape's geometry (`IWorldQuery.Geometry`; a runtime object's at its current pose; the ground's plane from the pair, on the map's ground function the local plane of `SampleGround` at the contact). The pair lives while the shape cuts the disc's slab (tested from the cached geometry each step, no query), so a rod that passes the rim into the disc is still met.
   - **The cut:** the shape inflated by the blade's half-thickness envelope (drone.json's disc half-thickness, 4 mm) is cut by the disc's plane: a plane gives a half-plane, a box a convex polygon, a sphere a disc, a capsule or cylinder a stadium or ellipse-bounded region, a wire its chain's stadia (closed forms, √ only). For a blade at azimuth ψ its radial line meets the region first at r_in(ψ).
   - **The passes:** each blade's azimuth sweeps from the last step's `RotorAngle` to this one's (at most 120° a step at 20 000 rpm); a pass is a blade sweeping across the region's azimuth range. It meets it at r_m, the smallest r_in over the swept range (the region's nearest point if its azimuth is swept, else the range's ends), with the face's normal n there, the blade's velocity there (ω × r plus the hub's), its normal speed into the face u_n and the face's tilt to the disc, sin β = |n·axis|. Only blade material beyond r_m and inside x_tip meets the face.

5. **Who yields, and how** (the hardness rule; the modes). Each row of `physics_materials.json` and each soil row of `surfaces.json` gains `hardness_pa`, `crush_strength_pa` and `density_kg_m3` (asset-pipeline's new requirement; values published by material, labelled in 1.1: structural steel's Brinell order 1.2–1.6 GPa, concrete's and masonry's quartz and cement far above the blade, timber's side-grain hardness tens of MPa and its compression across the grain 4–10 MPa, the Wood Handbook). **Rabinowicz's rule** (published: abrasion is severe once the counterface is harder than the worn surface, mild below; Rabinowicz 1995): a face harder than the blade (H > H_b) is a **hard target**, the blade yields; a softer one is a **soft target**, the target yields.
   - **Hard target, bend or crush.** The blade can clear a tilted face by bending flatwise: to clear the face at its tip it needs an axial deflection p·cot β, p the tip's depth past the face along n, against its rotating flap stiffness k_f (from p7b's K_β at the rotor's speed, derived), a force k_f·p·cot β; crushing back costs σ_c·A(r_m) (the section at the face). The smaller wins (assumed: the blade takes the path of least resistance).
     - **Crush (the trim and the chip):** the blade beyond r_m is crushed back to r_m in the pass (a face parallel to the axis: the vertical wall beside a level disc). Work E = σ_c·V (V the blade's volume crushed) plus the friction work μ·F_n along the tip's slide over the face (μ the row's, polymer on the face); the face pushes the hub along n with F_n = σ_c·A(r_m) over the contact time t_c = α/ω, cos α = r_m / r_tip (the tip enters the face at azimuth α and is crushed back by the closest approach; `DetMath.Acos`, a fixed polynomial added with its error bound tested); the tip slides r_tip·α along the face. At 1 mm into a vertical wall this is about 0.05–0.1 N·s a pass and 1–4 J (derived from the assumed σ_c and tip section; 1.3 prints them): a drone that drifts into a wall with its props is pushed off it, its rpm dips, and the tips shorten pass by pass.
     - **Break:** at each pass the face's force on the struck span (F_n, and the friction μ·F_n along the motion) bends every inner station; where M(x) = F·(r_c − x·R) first passes M_e or M_f (with the blade's drawn strength), the blade breaks there: everything beyond is lost, the work is G_c·A(x) plus the fragment's kinetic energy ½·m_frag·v_cm² (it leaves the rotor). Small breaks near the tip are chips; a break near the root loses the blade.
     - **Bend and slap:** the blade meets the face with its flatwise speed u_n/sin β against the tip's modal mass and flap stiffness, an elastic impact of peak F = u·√(k_f·m_tip) (derived; m_tip the first flap mode's modal mass at the tip), capped by the tip's local crush σ_c·(chord × contact width, assumed 1 mm); the section check above decides a break; a peak moment between the proportional limit and M_f leaves a flap set and a pitch set at that station (the plastic rotation (M − M_p)/k_p, k_p the section's elastic stiffness × 0.1, assumed); otherwise the blade slides over the face elastically, its friction work μ·k_f·p·cot β along the slide, and wears.
     - **Wear:** Archard's V = k·F_n·s/H_b (Archard 1953, published form) on the leading edge's stations in contact, k by the hardness ratio (severe against a harder face, published order 1e-3–1e-2 for polymers abraded by hard asperities, Lancaster 1969; assumed per class in 1.1): a long scrape sands the tips.
   - **Soft target, carve.** The blade carves the target at its own crushing pressure p_t (`crush_strength_pa`; a soil row's c·N_c, N_c 5.14, Prandtl's, published, from the row's cohesion) over the volume it sweeps, V = the span inside × the blade's frontal height in its motion (c·sin θ + t·cos θ, derived) × its path inside, and flings the mass it meets, m_f = ρ_t·V (p7a's form: a light body brought to the blade's speed costs m_f·v², half kept, half lost, derived). E = p_t·V + m_f·v². The blade is unharmed unless the cutting force E/(path) breaks a section (the check above), and the target is not changed (assumed: the world is static; a carved groove is not stored). In soil the flung mass dominates: a 2 mm graze costs about 2.4 J a pass and a 10 mm dig about the rotor's whole energy (derived at 1500 kg/m³): props that touch the ground bog down, and dig in and stop. Soil's sand abrades the blade by Archard with the quartz's hardness (Lancaster's severe class).
   - **Compliant thin rows** (straw, foliage, twig shells, fabric, mesh) take no blade damage here: their plants' cutting is p7a's elements (decision 7); fabric and mesh wrapping a prop are 7d's.

6. **What the strike does in its step, and the crash rule.**
   - **The rotor:** each pass's energy E leaves the rotor in the strike's step, ω' = √(ω² − 2E/J) (as p7a); with 2E ≥ Jω² the rotor stops on the face (`RotorJammed`, shared with 7a), and the pass's damage is cut short: a crush reaches only E_avail/(σ_c·A) of its depth (the chip's size given the energy), a carve keeps the rest of its work for the next turn. The motor turns it up again by its own model, so a prop pressed into a trunk or the ground sticks "a bit" and strikes again (D-059).
   - **The body:** the opposite angular impulse J·(ω − ω') about the rotor's axis, the tangential impulse J·(ω − ω')/r_c at the meeting point along the blade's motion, and the face's push along n (crush: F_n·t_c; carve: the pressure on the blade's pitched face, the tangential impulse × cot θ along the axis, derived). The pass's in-plane force at the hub (its impulse over t_c) is the shaft's and bearings' load (decision 9).
   - **The order:** strikes are sensed after the move (`Sense`), their mechanics change the state's rotor speeds and body velocity in that step (as p7a's), the damage they leave is written to the blade and motor tables, and the rotor's coefficients act from the next step (deterministic order: p7a's element strikes, then shape strikes by pair order, then the damage).
   - **The crash:** a prop strike is no longer a crash (`ContactSpec.CrashPropSpeed` retired). The flight ends on the body's acceleration (50 g) as before, so a 10 m/s hit on `H01_D`'s wall still ends in a crash when the body meets it, now named by the body part, with the prop's strike and damage before it in the log.

7. **The vegetation seam** (`p7a-ground` design 9). 7f reads each element strike from `BladeStrikes` in its step: its cut energy E_cut, its diameter d, its kind and its radius r_strike on the blade (open question 2: 7a's slot as designed holds E, the impulse, the kind and the diameter; 7f needs E_cut and r_strike too). The cutting force is E_cut/d (the cut's work over its travel, derived), acting along the blade's motion at r_strike: the edgewise section check (decision 5) decides a chip or a break; elements are softer than the blade, so nothing is crushed. A 3 mm straw's few newtons never break a section (derived from 7a's cut-energy order); a 20 mm green twig's hundreds can (derived). 7f adds shape strikes to the same slot, so 7d and 7e read one list.

8. **The motor's bell, its gap and binding** (`MotorDamage`; `drone.json`'s new `motors[].internals`, physics-owned, asset-artist reviewing).
   - **The bell's band:** a motor sphere's contact whose point lies between the base's top (6 mm, measured T) and the bell's top belongs to the bell, which turns with the rotor (derived from the motor's geometry in `drone.json`).
   - **The scrape:** the bell's surface moves at ω × (p − axis); a contact on the bell slides on its relative velocity, kinetic friction μ_k·F_n (aluminium on the row, assumed per row in 1.1) along it: the rotor feels −μ_k·F_n·r_bell (spin-signed), the body the reaction, a push along the wall. While the bell turns the stick anchor is not used (it slips); a stopped bell keeps the anchor. So a motor scraped on a wall slows, draws more current and drags the drone along the wall (the pilot's "scratch the motors").
   - **The dent:** the band as a thin ring of the aluminium can and the steel back iron (thicknesses and yields assumed in 1.1); its plastic collapse under a radial load P_c = 4M_p/R_bell (DeRuntz and Hodge 1963, a ring crushed between plates, published; the single load against the shaft's support taken as equivalent, assumed), M_p = Σ σ_y·b·t²/4. A contact's peak normal force F > P_c sets a dent δ_p += (F − P_c)/k_h (k_h the ring's elastic radial stiffness × 0.1, assumed) at that azimuth of the bell, and P_c rises to F (work hardening, assumed). Logged `MotorDamaged` (dent).
   - **The gap and Kt:** the gap there is g − δ_p, g 0.3 mm (assumed, typical of the class); the air-gap flux of a surface magnet B = B_r/(1 + μ_r·g/l_m) (Hanselman 2006, published form; B_r and μ_r of sintered NdFeB, published; l_m 1.5 mm assumed), averaged over the dent's arc (a quarter of the circumference, assumed) and the rest, gives Kt (= Ke) its ratio to new (derived: a dent to a third of the gap raises Kt about 3 %; a light dent's change is small, and the harm is the rub past the gap).
   - **The rub:** past the gap the dented magnets press the stator with N = k_r·(δ_p − g), k_r the thin ring's radial stiffness under opposed point loads, δ = 0.149·P·R³/(EI) (Roark's, Young and Budynas 2002, published), and drag the rotor with μ_s·N·r_stator (μ_s 0.3, assumed, nickel coating on lacquered laminations). The rub wears both by Archard (decision 5's form; k assumed), so the interference falls and the magnet thins (l_m down, so B and Kt down): a scraped, dented motor runs rough and weak, and grinds in.
   - **Binding:** a rub torque plus the motor's losses beyond Kt·I_lim (0.42 N·m at 40 A, derived) stops the rotor: `MotorBound`, the ESC holding its limit into a stalled winding (the existing model: d' = I_lim·R/V); it turns again only if the rub wears below the drive.

9. **The shaft and the bearings.**
   - **The loads:** the hub's in-plane load from a strike (the pass's impulse over t_c) and the bell's contact force, at their heights above the upper bearing (from `motors[].internals`: the prop's hub about 16 mm above, the bell's band 4–20 mm, the bearings 12 mm apart; all assumed from the motor's 32 mm height and its 15 mm stator); statics on two bearings give the shaft's moment at the upper bearing, M = F·ℓ, and the bearings' radial loads R₁ = F·(1 + ℓ/s), R₂ = F·ℓ/s (derived).
   - **A bent shaft:** past M_y = σ_y·πd³/32 (d 5 mm, published; hardened steel σ_y 1.2–1.8 GPa, assumed; 15–22 N·m, derived) the shaft takes a plastic hinge rotation φ_p += (M − M_y)/k_φ (k_φ its elastic 3EI/ℓ × 0.05, assumed) in the load's direction, fixed in the rotating frame. It moves the prop's centre by φ_p·ℓ_prop (mass unbalance), tilts the disc by φ_p (a couple: the prop's centrifugal moment I·ω²·φ_p for the tilt in its blades' plane, derived; and the thrust tilted, an in-plane T·φ_p turning at 1/rev, derived). Past the ultimate moment (σ_u, the round section's plastic shape factor 1.7, published) the shaft breaks and the prop is lost (`PropLost`: thrust 0, J the bell's).
   - **Brinelled bearings:** past C0r (276 N, published) a bearing's races take a permanent dent δ_b = 1e-4·D_w·(R/C0r)² (Palmgren's δ ∝ Q² anchored at ISO 76's 1e-4·D_w at C0, derived; D_w from the bearing's published geometry). Its friction M = ½·μ_b·P·d (SKF's constant-coefficient model, μ_b 0.0015, published) grows by the dent's share (assumed: doubling at ISO 76's dent): a tiny drag, logged. Its ball-pass frequencies (BPFO, BPFI, from the published geometry, derived) carry a force of the rotor's mass × (2πf)²·δ_b; most lie above the step's 500 Hz Nyquist, so it never enters the 1 kHz body (it would alias): it is published as its RMS into `CameraVibration`'s bands (an output only, derived), for `p7e-camera-feel`.

10. **The ESC, the current and the OSD.** Propulsion's motor takes each motor's Kt ratio, its added drag torques (rub, bearing, scrape) beside Kt·I0, and the rotor's J; the ESC and the bus are unchanged. A dragging motor draws more current at the same thrust, and its ESC reaches the 40 A limit sooner, so PHY-10's limit-driven shake (D-051) comes easier on a damaged drone. A trimmed prop needs more power for the same thrust (a smaller disc), so even a clean trim raises the hover current (derived). The OSD's current element (D-057, `p4-feed` 1.5) reads `MotorCurrentTotalA`, so it shows all of this with no new element; rpm is not on the pilot's OSD (OSD-03), and each motor's rpm and current are in the log and the summary.

11. **Breakage, in one list:** a blade broken at a station (decision 5, 7), a blade lost at its root, a prop lost with its shaft (decision 9), a rotor jammed (decision 6), a motor bound (decision 8). Each is an event with its cause's strike; the physics flies the result: one blade gone is an unbalance of about 0.4 kg·mm at 0.05 m from the hub, hundreds of newtons at hover speed (derived), and half the rotor's thrust, so the drone cannot hold its attitude.

12. **Randomness** (PHY-24): two new streams, both bounded perturbations of named physical parameters, drawn at the flight's start and logged with their bounds:
    - `prop.blade_pitch` (decision 3): eight Δθ, uniform in ±0.25° (assumed).
    - `prop.blade_strength`: each blade's σ_f, σ_c and G_c × (1 + δ), δ nearly normal, σ 5 %, clamped ±12 % (assumed: moulded glass-filled nylon varies with fibre orientation and weld lines; no spread is published for props). So the same strike chips a little more or less, from the blade's own strength; the chip's size given the energy follows (decision 6). Nothing about the strike, the world or the damage is drawn.

13. **Persistence, the log and the summary.**
    - **State:** the blade and motor tables are preallocated with the flight (like `ContactModel`'s latches), a function of the flight so far; they reset only at a new flight.
    - **The step record** gains per rotor: η_T and η_Q at the step's J, the unbalance (two components in the rotor's frame), the aerodynamic 1/rev moment and in-plane force amplitudes and phases, the couple, J, the Kt ratio, the added drag torque and the flags (jammed, bound, blade lost, prop lost). They are constant between events, so they deflate to almost nothing; LOG-01's 50 MB a minute is re-measured.
    - **Events:** `BladeDamaged` (mode: crush, chip, break, bend, wear; rotor, blade, station range, mass lost, energy, the strike's object, shape and material), `BladeLost`, `PropLost`, `MotorDamaged` (dent, rub, shaft, bearing; its size), `MotorBound`, `RotorJammed` and `PropStrike` gaining the shape strike's fields (r_m, u_n, β, the mode, E). Appended to the kinds, never renumbered.
    - **The header:** `props.blade` and `motors[].internals` (with `drone.json`'s hash), the materials' new fields (under the materials hash), the two new streams' draws.
    - **`DamageReport`:** per rotor and blade, the final state and its events in order, built from the log by `tools/replay` as well as live; the post-flight summary lists it (game-developer); 7e's forensics names a crash's prior strikes from it.
    - **Replay:** `tools/replay` re-runs from the header and inputs (as for the filters), bit for bit.

14. **The acceptance harness** (`tools/flightbench --damage`; the pilot's words, D-024; the scripted `SimPilot`).
    - **The pilot's account, "motors worsening when you scratch the motors and/or propellers on a wall":** the drone flies along `H01_D`'s south wall at 1, 2 and 4 m/s with one prop's rim 1, 3 and 5 mm into it for 0.3 s, and again with a motor bell pressed on it at 5 and 20 N, then hovers 10 s; on 40 seeds a cell (the draws vary, the wall does not). ## Risks / Trade-offs
- **Assumed strengths and stiffnesses set the thresholds.** The crush stress, the flap stiffness, the ring's collapse load, the shaft's yield and the gap decide where a scrape becomes a trim, a chip, a break or a bind. Each is a named parameter with its published order; the harness prints each outcome's sensitivity to each (±30 %), and a target that misses means a missing mechanism, never a tuned threshold.
- **The hardness rule is a two-class split** of a continuous wear transition (Rabinowicz's band); timber against glass-filled nylon sits well inside "soft", concrete and steel well inside "hard", so the range's targets do not sit near the edge. Aluminium conductors (about 0.3 GPa) are near it; 1.1 reports where each row falls.
- **The bind is sensitive:** tens of µm of interference can pass 0.42 N·m with Roark's stiffness. Archard wear relieves it; the harness prints how long a rub takes to grind in.
- **BEMT's ratios are taken at 9000 rpm** and on a two-class tip loss; 1.2 compares with a 160-station run at 6000 and 13 000 rpm and reports the error.
- **No published measured thrust loss of a trimmed 10-inch prop was found;** the checks are APC's own diameters and Pose et al.'s qualitative results. A pilot's qualitative "does a chipped prop feel like that" at the MVP is the remaining check.
- **The factory pitch bound is assumed** and moves PHY-07's band and the baseline shake together; both are recomputed and stated.
- **Every golden with a prop strike moves** (1.6b's range cases and the contact flight): each is re-recorded once and audited against its parent with damage off.
- **A prop strike stops being a crash:** the player's flight now continues after a wall touch, which changes what ends a sortie; the summary and the crash report must say so.
