## Why
The pilot flew the map build and asked for "motors worsening when you scratch the motors and/or propellers on a wall" (D-056), and for props "getting stuck a bit" (D-059). Today a prop disc only detects: it carries no load, a strike on a rigid shape faster than 3 m/s ends the flight as a crash (`p6-flight-core` 1.6), and a slower one leaves the prop as new. Real props chip, shorten, bend and break; real motors scrape, dent, bind and run rough; and the drone flies on worse, shaking once a revolution, needing more throttle and more amps, until the pilot lands it or it falls. Every part of that must come from the strike's energy and geometry and the materials, never from a scripted loss or noise on the pose.

## What Changes
- **The blade as a structure** (PHY-26): each of the eight blades carries its own state along the sheet's stations (tip radius, chord lost, pitch and flap set, mass lost) from `drone.json`'s new `props.blade` block (glass-filled nylon's published modulus, strength, density and impact toughness; the sheet's chord and thickness).
- **A damaged blade's aerodynamics:** blade-element momentum theory on the damaged span gives each rotor's thrust and torque against the published tables' advance ratio, each blade's share and its centroid; the rotor's mass, inertia and unbalance follow the material lost. The hub feels a once-per-revolution force (the mass unbalance, U·ω²) and moment and in-plane force (the blades' unequal lift and drag), turning with the rotor's angle, into the rigid body, the frame's modes and the camera (`p7e-camera-feel` reads them through the pose and `VibrationBands`).
- **A new prop's factory imbalance** (PHY-07, D-059): the existing mass unbalance draw (`prop.unbalance`, ISO 21940-11's G 6.3) gains each blade's factory pitch offset (`prop.blade_pitch`, bounded, seeded, logged): the baseline 1/rev shake every flight starts from, and a per-rotor thrust offset that joins PHY-07's yank. PHY-07's band is recomputed.
- **Where a blade meets a shape:** each disc pair's exact geometry (`IWorldQuery.Geometry`) is cut by the disc's plane; every blade pass in the step (from `RotorAngle`) meets it at a radius, a speed and a face normal. Walls, poles, trunks, cars, the stand, conductors and the ground alike.
- **Who yields, by hardness** (Rabinowicz's hardness ratio): against a harder face (concrete, masonry, steel, glass, aluminium) the blade is crushed back (a trimmed or chipped tip), breaks where the impact's bending passes its section's strength, or, when the face is tilted to the disc, bends flatwise over it and slaps, chips or wears (Archard); a softer target (timber, bark, rubber, soil) is carved at its own crushing pressure plus the mass the blade flings, the blade unharmed unless the cutting force breaks it or the rotor runs out of energy and sticks.
- **What a strike does in its step:** the rotor loses the pass's energy (an rpm dip, or a stop: `RotorJammed`), the body takes the face's push and the tangential and angular impulses, and the blade's new state acts from the next step. **A prop strike is no longer a crash:** the drone flies on worse; a crash is the body's acceleration (50 g) as before.
- **The vegetation seam** (`p7a-ground` 1.15): 7f reads each element strike in `FlightState.BladeStrikes`; straw and leaves never chip a blade, a thick twig can.
- **Motor damage:** a spinning bell scraped on a wall takes the friction on its moving surface (a drag torque, more current, a push along the wall); a hard enough push dents the bell (thin-ring plastic collapse), narrowing the magnetic gap; past the gap the magnets rub the stator (a drag torque that wears in, a thinner magnet and a lower torque constant), and a rub stronger than the motor's torque at its ESC's current limit binds it (`MotorBound`). The hub's strike loads bend the shaft past its yield (a run-out and a tilted disc: mass and couple unbalance, a tilted thrust) and brinell the bearings past their static rating (ISO 76: a drag torque and a ball-pass vibration).
- **The ESC and the current react through the existing model:** more drag means more current and the 40 A limit sooner (PHY-10's cause, D-051); the OSD's current element (D-057) shows it with no new element; rpm is not on the pilot's OSD (OSD-03).
- **Breakage:** a blade broken at a station, a blade lost at its root, a shaft past its ultimate strength (the prop lost), a motor bound or jammed: each a logged event; the physics flies the result (a lost blade throws the drone over).
- **Persistence:** the damage stays for the flight, in the step record (per rotor's aggregates), the events and a `DamageReport` the post-flight summary lists; a replay rebuilds it bit for bit.
- **The acceptance harness** (`tools/flightbench --damage`): the pilot's account ("worse after a scratch") from scripted scrapes on the range's wall, pole, car and trunk; published checks (APC's tables, the trimmed-prop bracket, Pose et al.'s symmetric and one-tip cuts, ISO grades); each target failing a known-wrong model.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `prop-motor-damage`: the blade's structure and state, the damaged rotor's aerodynamics and unbalance, the factory imbalance, the blade-meets-shape geometry, the hardness rule and the strike's modes, the strike's mechanics on the rotor and body, the vegetation seam, the motor's bell, gap, shaft and bearings, the ESC and the OSD current, breakage, persistence and the summary, and the randomness, determinism, budget and log they keep

### Modified Capabilities
- `asset-pipeline`: ADDED, the physics material rows say what a blade meets (`hardness_pa`, `crush_strength_pa`, `density_kg_m3`, each labelled), checked by `meta.validate`. A new requirement rather than a change to "Physics material rows are per touching part", which `p7a-ground` modifies, so the two archives never clash.

## Non-goals
- **Drawing the damage:** the props in the feed (dark arcs and blurred blades, `drone.md`) and in the chase view keep their look; a trimmed or lost blade drawn is a follow-up for the tech-artist and asset-artist (open question 1).
- **Heat:** the motors' and ESCs' temperature, a bound motor burning out, an ESC's desync and stall protection (firmware-specific; open question 3).
- **Arms, plates, legs and the frame breaking:** out of this change (PHY-26 names props and motors).
- **A stopped prop's blades as load-bearing contact bodies:** a disc still carries no contact force while it does not turn (today's rule); a strike needs a turning rotor.
- **Wires wrapping a prop** (`p7d-tether`, PHY-22): a conductor is a hard shape for the blade's damage here; its wrap, pull and flip are 7d's. Thin flexible sheets (fabric, mesh) that would wrap a prop join 7d's soft-body class (D-059).
- **The frame's in-plane and torsion modes:** the 1/rev loads drive the existing modes by their inertial relief; whether the arms' torsion and in-plane bending near 1/rev need modes of their own is `p7e-camera-feel`'s (open question 4).
- **Damage carried between flights:** each flight starts on new props and sound motors.
- **New OSD elements:** OSD-03's layout is the footage's; no rpm element is added.
