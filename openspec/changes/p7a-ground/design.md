# Design: p7a-ground

## Context
- **What exists** (`p6-flight-core` 1.6, 1.6b; `p6-world-query-gallery` 1.4–1.5): each corner's leg is one spring and damper (10 kN/m assumed, 45 N·s/m derived) carrying its skid's points by depth; the feet are skids, a carbon tube of radius 0.005 m (measured) running 0.22 m (measured) from the skid's middle to its end on each corner, with support spheres at the end and the T-joint; body pairs take the shape's row stiffness in series with the part; friction is a stick anchor (tangential stiffness 0.5 × normal, assumed); the ground is rigid with one pair μ 0.5/0.4 for every surface; the dispatch is on `Surface != 0`; the skid's same-face rule and the seam rule hold coplanar pieces to one contact; a compliant shape passed through is no tunnel.
- **The map:** a flat plane (`belt_straw`), absent over S04's 46 patches (a soil box `surface:belt_straw`, 195 compliant `straw` boxes 0.125 m deep, three `crater_spoil` mounds) and inside the hole bands (E01's fillers, Z04's shaft). `GroundSample` has height, normal, surface and the `Hole` flag only. No micro-detail query; the stand is a static runtime object (W8).
- **The budget** (1.6b's bench, AC): resting in S04's straw, p99 260 µs a step, its queries 194 µs; the field stand's launch p99 136 µs (D-054).
- **The rule:** randomness only as a bounded perturbation of a named physical parameter from a named, logged stream; the ground and cover are world-derived and never drawn.
- **D-059** (the pilot after the map build): "straws and leaves still dont affect flight physics ... add wobbling, shaking, props getting stuck a bit". Today a prop disc entering a `twig` shell is a strike event with no load, and S04's straws, stems and twigs and the crowns' leaves are drawn only.

## Goals / Non-Goals
**Goals:** PHY-01…06 physical, logged and replayable on the test range; the pilot's account reproduced from causes, each statistical target failing a known-wrong model; determinism, 0 B and PLT-04 kept.
**Non-Goals:** the proposal's.

## Decisions

1. **The surfaces table** (`assets-src/surfaces.json`, physics-owned, the schema and label rules of `physics_materials.json`). Rows for the thirteen surfaces (S01–S13), each with three sets, `dry`, `wet` and `saturated`:
   - **Soil:** Bekker's linear pressure–sinkage, p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·z (Bekker 1969, n = 1), with b the part's diameter (derived: the conforming groove's width) so it needs no power; the legacy bearing at b_ref 0.015 m (K§7.2, assumed) split by a per-surface cohesive share (assumed). Unload ratio, `max_sink_m`, the firm layer 1e8 N/m³ (carried, assumed), viscous damping (N·s/m³, carried); μs/μk of a polymer or carbon part on the soil (Potyondy 1961's interface ratios, values carried and labelled); adhesion σ_a and interface adhesion c_a (Pa) with a release separation δ_a (m), zero when dry; the soil lip's edge radius for 7d (0.02 m, assumed).
   - **Cover kinds** (straw, stem, twig, leaf; D-059): E (wheat straw 4.8–6.6 GPa, O'Dogherty et al. 1995, published; weed stems assumed; green twigs the Wood Handbook's green moduli, published), the hollow wall's share of the diameter (assumed), the density (green wood, the Wood Handbook, published; dry straw assumed), a leaf's mass per area (the published 30–120 g/m² of temperate broadleaves, Wright et al. 2004; the value assumed), the hook release range (N, carried from surfaces-review §3, assumed), the specific cut energy e_s (J/m², the published order of straw and stalk shearing studies, Persson 1987; value per kind assumed; a leaf's from its petiole), the roller's cross-axis μ (0.05, carried, assumed).
   - **Wet and saturated** (D-021): starting from the dry set, bearing ×0.3 / ×0.15, unload ratio ×1.5, max sink ×2, μ ×0.8 / ×0.6, σ_a 3 / 5 kPa, δ_a 2 / 3 mm (all assumed; the published direction and order: Bekker's moduli fall severalfold to an order of magnitude as a clay wets, tabulated in Wong 2008; soil–metal friction passes from friction to adhesion to lubrication as moisture rises, Nichols 1931; normal adhesion of the order of kPa, Fountaine 1954; breakout suction under a lifted object, Vesic 1971, Foda 1982). Wet cover: hook release ×1.5, e_s ×1.5, E ×0.7 (assumed; wetter straw is tougher and softer, the published direction of straw moisture studies). Task 1.1 pins each value from its source, or labels it assumed with its reason.
   - **The new rows:** `dirt_track` (compacted silt loam), `asphalt` and `concrete_slab` (rigid, μ only), derived in 1.1.
   - **Logged:** the rows in use and the file's hash in the header (as `physics_materials.json`'s); replay builds from the header and refuses a mismatched hash.
   - **`physics_materials.json` gains:** `area_law` (`mat`, `punch` or `winkler`) and `area_reference_depth_m` on `straw`, `fabric` and `sandbag`; `elastic_modulus_pa` on `bark` (green black locust, the Wood Handbook's value, published) and `deadwood` (dry hardwood derated for checking and decay, assumed, as its break stress is); a `wet` block on `straw` (stiffness ×0.6, μ, σ_a 0.3 kPa, all assumed).

2. **The soil under a part** (PHY-01, K§7.1 rewritten for the skids). Winkler's foundation: the soil's force is k times the volume the part displaces below the soil surface.
   - **Virgin loading:** F_l = k_l·V(z). A half-skid's section displaces A(z) = (2/3)·w·z with the exact chord w = 2√(2rz − z²) up to z = r (derived: a parabolic segment on the true chord, within 15 % of the circular segment, 6 % at z = r/2), then A(r) + 2r(z − r); V = ∫A along the half-skid by a fixed three-point Simpson rule (its middle end, its middle, its outer end). A sphere (the coil, a support on a box) displaces a cap, V = πz²(r − z/3) (published geometry). Only + − × ÷ √.
   - **Unloading and reloading** (Wong's repetitive-loading rule, Wong 2008): the groove conforms to the part, so F_u = k_u·S_m·(z − z_p), k_u = unload ratio × k_l, S_m the footprint at the deepest sink z_m (a half-skid's w_m × its length; a sphere's π(2r z_m − z_m²)), z_p fixed by F_u(z_m) = F_l(z_m). F = max(0, min(F_l, F_u)); past `max_sink_m` the firm layer adds 1e8·S·(z − max_sink); damping c_s·S·ż.
   - **In series with the leg:** per corner each step the leg's deflection x solves k_leg·x = F_soil(δ_i − x), δ_i the undeflected overlap at each Simpson point, by four Newton steps from the last step's x (assumed count; the tests hold the residual under 1e-9 N), then the dampers act on the rates as now. The force acts at the soil's load centroid along the half-skid. On a rigid shape the P7 branch stays (the leg alone); on a compliant row decision 6.
   - **Where the depth comes from:** on a shape's face, the contact's plane gives every Simpson point's depth (exact on a flat face); on the ground function, each point is a `SampleGround` sample (12 a step, from 8).
   - **What it gives** (checked in 1.1 and 1.4, not asserted here): a corner's 12.7 N share (5.18 kg, derived) on dry `belt_straw` sinks about 2 mm, on wet about 4 mm, on saturated about 6 mm (at a cohesive share of one half); a 15 mm round foot's legacy sinks (K§7.2) are reproduced at 25 N by the same rows.

3. **Friction, ploughing and footprints.**
   - **The anchor** (K§7.1 step 5, kept): per half-skid at its load centroid, tangential stiffness 0.5 × the series normal stiffness (assumed). It holds up to μs·Fn + c_a·S_m + P(θ) and slides at μk·Fn + c_a·S_m + P(θ) (Coulomb with interface adhesion, τ = c_a + σ·tan δ, published).
   - **Ploughing is the groove's passive resistance:** P(θ) = ½·k_l·z_m²·ℓ(θ), ℓ(θ) = L·|sin θ| + 2r·|cos θ|, θ between the anchor's stretch and the skid's axis (derived: the Winkler pressure, zero at the surface and k_l·z at the foot, on the frontal face; K§7.1's kl·b·δ² without the ½ doubles it). So a sunk skid resists sideways about L/2r ≈ 22 times more than along its axis (derived), and a glide landing with forward speed slides along its skids but trips when it yaws or drifts.
   - **Footprints:** a ring of eight grooves (assumed: two per corner), each with its soil's identity (a ground-function surface, or an (object, shape)), the half-skid's two ends, and z_m, z_p, w_m at each end. A half-skid touching down within w_m/2 across and 10° (assumed) of a print on the same soil starts from it, so a re-landing finds compacted soil. The world stores no marks.

4. **Uneven ground** (PHY-04).
   - **On the open ground,** the world's ground function (world-builder, `Runtime/World/`): the map's zones (decision 11) add hashed micro-relief, directional ridges and placed analytic features (pitfalls, small craters, ruts; WLD-11) to the plane, from `DetMath` alone; `GroundSample` gains `FeatureId` and `FeatureDepth`; the zones' floors are drawn from the same function's triangulation, and `SampleGround` returns the drawn triangle (WLD-11's 1 mm parity), so the pilot lands on what they see.
   - **Under S04's straw,** pits cut through the soil box with the mat boxes draped into them (asset-artist): visible as a slight hollow in the straw, felt as a drop. The metadata's `features` records name each pit's shapes; the world's `Feature(object, shape)` gives physics the pit's id.
   - **Logged:** `PitfallEntered` (corner, feature id, depth) at a Simpson point's first touch inside a feature. `WorldBug terrain_deep` when a point sinks 0.25 m (PHY-04, carried) past the ground function outside a hole; `WorldBug bottomless` when a support over a hole falls 2 m (assumed) below the plane with no soil shape found.
   - **Decided from PHY-04's deferred list:** debris chunks wait for a rubble ground (none on the range); roots in cut trench walls are fiber hooks (7d); the soil lip's edge radius is the table's 0.02 m.

5. **An asset's own soil shapes** (E01's review S16). A soil contact whose point lies inside another soil shape of the same object by more than the seam tolerance (1 µm) is buried and never acts. Of the rest on one half-skid, contacts whose normals lie within 45° of the deepest's merge into it, and the deepest sets the surface and the depth (WLD-16's rule, carried among an asset's own shapes). A face more than 45° off that is not buried (a trench wall) is its own pair. So E01's rings, 25 mm under the berm, never set the surface, and a buried box's side face never pushes a skid sideways; F-3 is not restated and E01 is not rebuilt.

6. **Compliant layers** (`p1-materials-per-part` S4; the plan row).
   - **Area laws:** a row's `stiffness_n_per_m` is for the reference part (the 15 mm foot) at `area_reference_depth_m`. `mat` (straw): k = E_m·A_s/D with the 2:1 spread area at the layer's depth D (K§7.1 step 4: a disc π((b + D)/2)², a capsule lying along the face (ℓ + D)(2r + D)) and E_m = k_ref·D_ref/A_s(ref) (derived; straw: 3.98 kPa against the table's 4 kPa); `punch` (fabric): k ∝ the footprint's equivalent diameter √(4A/π) (Sneddon's flat punch, published); `winkler` (sandbag): k ∝ the footprint area. A half-skid on S04's straw meets 3.03 × the reference foot's 490 N/m (derived), on fabric 3.5 ×, on a sandbag 12.4 ×.
   - **The stroke limit:** a compliant pair compresses at most 80 % of the shape's thickness along the contact normal (K§7.1's mat constant, assumed for every layer). Past it the layer is solid and carries the part onto what lies beneath: the same object's soil or rigid shape that the part's own query found below the layer (within the residual 20 % + 5 mm, assumed) takes the further sink by its own law; with none, the part's own spring (P7). At 160 N/m a 3–6 g landing no longer drives a foot 0.22–0.44 m into a mattress.
   - **Slender members:** a `bark` or `deadwood` capsule carrying a `cantilever` record (the chain length from its root to its end A, asset-artist) bends at k(x) = 3EI/x³ (Euler–Bernoulli, published), I = πr⁴/4, x the chain length to the contact; past 10 × the part's own spring (assumed) it is the rigid branch, exact. The root is taken as clamped (assumed: a limb's parent flexes too, so a twig on a limb reads stiffer than it is).

7. **Wet ground** (D-021). The rain level (Sunny, Rain, Severe rain) is in `FlightSetup` and the header (`p7c-wind` 1.7 adds it with the rain rate; whichever lands first). Sunny takes the dry set, Rain the wet, Severe rain the saturated (D-021: "Sunny means dry").
   - **Adhesion and suction:** once a part has loaded wet soil, its wetted area S_m holds it as it lifts: a cohesive law, the pull σ_a·S_m·(1 − s/δ_a) for a separation 0 ≤ s < δ_a from the groove's set, then nothing, S_m cleared (Dugdale 1960 and Barenblatt 1962's cohesive zone, published; the work to part is ½σ_a·δ_a·S_m, derived). It enters the series solve as a tension. A wet corner's 12.7 N share sinks a half-skid about 4 mm with a 2.2e-3 m² footprint, so the hold is about 6.5 N wet and 11 N saturated (derived from the assumed σ_a): of the order of the corner's weight share, which is why a leg sticks "most of the time in rain".
   - **Wet straw:** the `straw` row's `wet` block (softer, clinging with its small σ_a), the elements' wet release force and cut energy.

8. **Vegetation on the body and legs: stems, straws, crowns and hooks** (PHY-03, PHY-05; K§7.3; D-059: "a straw or two even tho they are tiny may affect the flight").
   - **The elements are the drawn ones** (AST-10, REAL-05): S04's generator writes its top-layer straws, standing stems and twigs as records; V01's and V13's generators write the twigs and leaflets their crowns draw, each twig with the parent it grows from (asset-artist; the Godot stand-in trees' drawn leaves too if open question 2 allows). The world's `MicroDetailNear(centre, radius, kinds, span)` returns them per placed instance from a 3-D cell index, with stable `Id`s (the instance and the element's index), in canonical order, with the true count (world-builder). S04's body straws stay the mat's compliance, never hooks.
   - **Crowns:** a crown twig is a cantilever rooted where it leaves its parent (the stems' law below), so a body pushing through a crown meets each twig in turn: drag and step-to-step jitter. Where an object's crown has elements, its `twig` shell pieces no longer push (their elements do; the shell stays for 7d's snags and the audit), so nothing counts twice. A leaf acts on the props (decision 9); its own push on the body, a leaflet's mass (about 0.02 g, derived from 4 cm² at the assumed mass per area) times the closing speed, is under 3e-4 N·s at 10 m/s (derived) and is left out.
   - **Per-element mechanics** come from the surfaces table by kind (and, for ground cover, the patch's surface), each element's value drawn by its own `Id`'s hash within the row's range (SplitMix64, world-derived, never a flight draw).
   - **The cache:** around the drone at r = 2 m (carried), refreshed when the centre has moved 0.5 m (carried) while the drone is within 1 m of an element-bearing instance's bounds, on the flight's worker, swapped in exactly 20 steps after the request (api-review §6); a late worker is a logged hitch, never another result. It holds to about 50 m/s (derived: the 2 m radius less the drone's 0.45 m reach and the 0.5 m refresh, over the 20 ms swap). Capacity 8192 elements (assumed: about twice S04's density over the 2 m disc; re-derived in 1.14 from the densest crown); overflow is a `WorldBug` and the canonical prefix is used. Each step tests the drone's parts against the elements in a local 0.25 m grid (carried).
   - **Standing stems:** rooted cantilevers, F = 3EI/h³·d at contact height h for a push d across the stem's line, capped where the push passes half the contact height (slide-off, carried, assumed) and at Euler's π²EI/(4L²) along the stem (published); ζ 0.1 (assumed). This is the touchdown jitter and the drag when skimming.
   - **Twigs under a skid:** a rigid roller on the mat (the twig's footprint through the `straw` area law beneath), friction 0.05 across its axis and the row's along it.
   - **Hooks, by which side the element lies on:** at a part's first touch of an anchored element, the element lies over the part if its closest point is above the part's core; the side is latched for that (element, part) pair while they touch. Only an over element can hook: a spring from the attach point back toward the element's base, reaching its release force R over 30 mm (carried), released at R (logged as a slip, a pull-out or a break by kind) or when the part moves back. So a vertical landing presses straws down and hooks almost none; a glide landing slides a skid's end under lofted straw ends and stems, and those can hold at liftoff. Up to 32 hooks (assumed), keyed by (`Id`, part), surviving a cache swap; overflow is a `WorldBug`.

9. **Prop strikes on vegetation** (PHY-05, X-6 decided; D-059: "props getting stuck a bit").
   - **The strike:** an element crossing a disc between the hub and the tip while the rotor turns is struck at the first blade pass after it enters (from `RotorAngle`, the two blades), at the blade's speed there, v = ω·r_strike.
   - **What it costs the rotor** comes from the element's own mechanics: E = E_cut + m_f·v². E_cut = e_s·A_section (hollow straw and stems, solid twigs, a leaf's petiole). m_f is the mass the blade brings to its own speed: a leaflet whole, or a stem's, straw's or twig's mass within one blade chord of the strike (assumed); a light body struck by a far heavier blade costs the blade m_f·v², half kept as the body's kinetic energy and half lost in the impact (derived). At the tip's 120 m/s (9000 rpm, derived) a 0.02 g leaflet costs about 0.3 J and a 3 mm straw about 0.3–0.5 J (most of it the flung mass), against the rotor's 27 J (½Jω², drone.json's 6.0e-5 kg·m², derived): a straw takes about 1 % of the rotor's speed in one step.
   - **The torque spike and the rpm dip:** the rotor's speed drops in the strike's step to ω' = √(ω² − 2E/J), J the rotor's inertia (drone.json); the body takes the opposite angular impulse J(ω − ω') about the rotor's axis and the in-plane impulse J(ω − ω')/r_strike at the strike point, along the blade's motion (derived). The motor and ESC model brings the rotor back over its own time constant (τ_m 43 ms, `p6-flight-core` 1.7), so a straw is a tick of yaw and shake, a tuft of stems a visible dip, through the loop's own response.
   - **The stall:** when ½Jω² < E the blade stops on the element (`RotorJammed`), the element keeps its remaining cut energy, and the motor's next turns strike again until it is cut: a prop "stuck a bit" in a thick twig or a dense clump.
   - **After the cut:** the element is shortened at the disc's plane for the rest of the flight (a leaf is gone), kept in a 2048-entry cut table (assumed; overflow a `WorldBug`).
   - **The seam with `p7f-damage`** (PHY-26): this change computes and applies the strike's mechanics and never damages a blade. Each strike is written in the step to `FlightState.BladeStrikes` (per rotor and blade: the energy E, its impulse, the element's kind and diameter) and logged as `PropStrike` (the element's `Id`, kind and diameter, E_cut, m_f, the rotor's energy before, ω', r_strike, the blade). `p7f-damage` reads `BladeStrikes` in the same step to chip, bend or break the blade, and adds strikes on shapes (walls, trunks) to the same slot.

10. **The launch stand as a free body** (PHY-06, D-022, W8: the pose-update option). The stand is a rigid body, 1.6 kg (derived, drone.json), its inertia from its six tubes (derived), stepped by semi-implicit Euler at 1 kHz with the drone. Its base bars meet the ground by decision 2 (each half-bar a flat line contact, the square tube's 0.02 m face displacing A = 0.02·z) and decision 3; the drone's parts meet its posts through `StaticContacts` as now, and the stand takes each pair's reaction at the contact point. After each step physics moves the runtime object to the stand's pose (`IWorldQuery.MoveObject`, world-builder: runtime objects only, between steps, on the physics thread, outside the content hash), so every query (the parts, the rotor rays, 7d's fiber) sees it where it is. Steel on soil takes the soil's polymer μ (assumed). An arm can catch or slide on a post, an uneven push tips the horizon, and a caught stand tips or drags.

11. **The test range's ground** (world-builder; D-047). Three zones of about 30 × 30 m (assumed sizes, placed near the start points so the pilot meets them):
    - **a firm dirt track** (`dirt_track`) under the track start, with two wheel ruts;
    - **a tilled field** (`tilled`) beside the field start, furrows of 0.05 m half-height at 0.5 m spacing (K§7.2, carried) across the usual heading, its pitfalls by K§7.2's rate;
    - **a shelled patch** (`crater_spoil`) with small craters (1–3 m, WLD-11's placed features) and pitfalls (K§7.2: 0.02 /m², 0.05–0.2 m deep, 0.1–0.4 m radius).
    The zones' relief and features are map data (`placements.json`), their mechanics the table's. Each point has one surface; the relief feathers to the plane at a zone's edge (C⁰). The floors use existing ground materials as stand-ins (D-040). S04's verges and E01 stay as they are, with S04's hidden pits.

12. **The leg's spread** (PHY-24). One stream `leg.stiffness`: each leg's k × (1 + δ), δ nearly normal, σ 3 %, clamped to ±10 % (assumed: the clamps' and T-joints' fit; nothing is published), four draws at the flight's start, in the header with their bounds. It changes which corner leaves last; nothing about the ground is drawn.

13. **The acceptance harness** (`tools/flightbench --ground`; the pilot's account, D-024).
    - **Liftoffs:** at 100 spots of each ground class (dry firm: the dirt track; dry soft: the plane's `belt_straw`, the tilled field and S04's verges), in Calm and Windy, dry, Rain and Severe rain; at each, half the spots a vertical landing at 0.3 m/s and half the pilot's glide (0.5 m/s down, 0.3 forward), the throttle cut, 2 s at rest, then a liftoff ramp to the hover + 0.1 over 0.5 s with the sticks held (assumed protocol). The spot sets the ground; the flight seed is the spot's index, so a spot's outcome changes only through the leg draws.
    - **A held leg:** a corner still carrying more than 1 N of ground load (supports, skid or hooks) at least 20 ms after the other three have left, while the attitude moves at least 0.5° toward it (assumed: 0.5° is about six of the feed's luma samples, D-049's 0.088°). Logged as `LegReleased` (corner, hold time, peak pull, cause: anchor, plough, adhesion or a hook's `Id`, the attitude change).
    - **The targets, read from the pilot's words** (the reading assumed, the words the pilot's): "sometimes, a little": on dry firm ground a held leg on at least one spot and at most a third, with smaller tips than on soft ground; "more often on softer ground": dry soft above dry firm by more than the two rates' 95 % binomial margin, and under half (it is "more often", and "most" is kept for rain); "most of the time in rain": soft ground in Rain above half, firm ground in Rain above dry firm, and Severe rain at least as often as Rain.
    - **The known-wrong models, each failing a target:** a dice roll at the dry firm rate everywhere (fails "more often" and "in rain"); the dry rows in rain (fails "in rain"); K§7.3's touch rule, every anchored element touching a skid hooks (fails "under half" on dry soft); Coulomb friction with no anchor, no adhesion and no ploughing (fails "at least one spot" and "in rain").
    - **The rest of PHY-02…06** runs as scenarios on the range's zones (the spec).

14. **Determinism, the budget and the log.**
    - **State, preallocated:** per half-skid z, z_m, z_p, w_m, S_m, the separation; per corner the leg's x; the footprint ring; the hook and cut tables; the element cache's two buffers; the stand's body. 0 B in steady state. Only + − × ÷ √ (`DetMath`).
    - **The step record** gains the half-skids' soil state, the legs' x, the hook count and the first four hooks' `Id` and force, and the stand's pose and velocity, and `BladeStrikes`; the events `PitfallEntered`, `HookAttached`, `HookReleased`, `LegReleased`, `RotorJammed`, `StandTipped`, `StandSlid` (5 mm, assumed), `WorldBug` and `PropStrike`'s new fields. The header gains the surfaces rows and hash, the rain level, the leg draws and the cache's parameters. LOG-01's 50 MB a minute is re-measured on the 120 s scripted flight.
    - **The budget** (PLT-04: p99 ≤ 300 µs, 0 B), judged on physics' pattern (D-054): resting in S04's straw with the cache, a landing into S04's stems, a hover with the props in V01_L's crown, the field stand's launch with the free stand, a landing on the tilled field. The straw rest already spends 260 µs at p99, 194 of them in the queries, so the world's broad phase inside each object (D-054) lands first (task 1.2).

## Risks / Trade-offs
- **The pilot's account may not come out.** If the physics gives no held leg on the dry track, the cause is a missing mechanism (a twig, a lip, the drift at liftoff), never a tuned rate. QA and the orchestrator decide with the log's causes in hand; nothing is drawn to fill the gap.
- **Assumed numbers dominate the wet hold:** σ_a and δ_a set "most of the time in rain". They are named parameters with published orders; the harness prints the hold rate's sensitivity to each.
- **The budget:** the straw rest is at 87 % of PLT-04 before stems; the element tests and the 12 samples add to it. Task 1.2 first, and the cache's grid bounds the tests.
- **The skid's section and Simpson's rule are approximations** (15 % at z = r, three points): 1.10 checks them against a 32-point rule and the exact segment, and reports the error at the resting sinks.
- **Most of the posadka's crowns are Godot stand-ins** (Z08–Z13, 494 placed against 161 V01 and V13): their collision is a timber skeleton and their foliage is drawn only. Without 1.8b the pilot meets D-059's crown effects only at the Unity trees and shrubs (open question 2).
- **Strikes on many elements a step** (a prop in a dense crown) cost element tests and rotor updates; the cache's grid and the disc's bounding test bound them, and 1.19 measures a hover in V01_L's crown.
- **Elements from a bake:** S04's records are its bake's; AST-10's shared generator, when the world grows, must produce the same elements or re-record the goldens.
- **Stand-in looks** on the zones (D-040): the ground behaves as tilled soil while it looks like the belt's soil.
- **Every physics task moves the range goldens.** Each is re-recorded once and audited against its parent with the new mechanism off.
